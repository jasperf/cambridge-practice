Paper Chromatography

How it works: a solvent soaks up the paper and carries the dyes with it. More soluble dyes travel further, so a mixture separates into spots at different heights.

Gas Particles & Pressure

Key idea: gas particles move quickly in all directions. They cause pressure by colliding with the walls of their container. Heating them makes them move faster → more collisions → more pressure.

It gets bigger (expands)

2 marks

The particles escape through the balloon into the water
The cold water was squeezing the balloon before

1 mark
The gas particles colliding with the walls

The gas dissolving into the walls
Gravity pulling the gas down
Q8

1 mark

It increases — faster particles hit the walls more often and harder
It stays the same
The gas turns into a solid
Q9

1 mark

Pressure = Force ÷ Area
Pressure = Area ÷ Force
Pressure = Area × Time

Concentration of Solutions

Words to know: the solute is what dissolves (e.g. sugar); the solvent is what it dissolves in (e.g. water); together they make a solution. More solute per volume = more concentrated.
