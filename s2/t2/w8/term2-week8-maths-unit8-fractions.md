## Section A: Fractions and Decimals

**Terminating decimal:** the division comes to an end, e.g. 3/4 = 0.75.

**Recurring decimal:** a digit or group of digits repeats forever, e.g. 2/3 = 0.666… = 0.​6̅.

**The denominator decides:** if the only prime factors of the denominator (in simplest form) are 2 and/or 5, the decimal terminates; otherwise it recurs.

**Class example:** 1/6 has denominator 6 = 2 × 3. The 3 makes it recur, so 1/6 = 0.1666… = 0.1​6̅. 3/20 has denominator 20 = 2² × 5, so it terminates: 3/20 = 0.15.

## Section B: Order of Operations with Fractions

**BODMAS still works with fractions.** The order is: Brackets, Orders (powers), Divide / Multiply (left to right), Add / Subtract (left to right). Mixed numbers are usually easiest changed to improper fractions first.

**Class example:** 2½ + ¾ × 2. Multiplication first: ¾ × 2 = 3/2. Then add: 2½ + 3/2 = 5/2 + 3/2 = 8/2 = 4.

## Section C: Multiplying Fractions

**Multiply the numerators and multiply the denominators.** It is usually easier to cancel common factors before you multiply. Mixed numbers must become improper fractions first. A whole number can be written as a fraction with denominator 1.

**Class example:** 2½ × 1⅗. Change to 5/2 × 8/5. Cancel the 5: 1/2 × 8/1 = 8/2 = 4.

## Section D: Dividing Fractions

**Turn the fraction you are dividing by upside down, then multiply.** This upside-down fraction is called the reciprocal. Cancel common factors before you multiply. Mixed numbers become improper fractions first.

**Class example:** 3/4 ÷ 2/5. The reciprocal of 2/5 is 5/2, so 3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8 = 1⅞.

## Section E: Making Calculations Easier

**Choose the easier form.** A calculation may contain fractions and decimals mixed together. Decide whether to rewrite everything as fractions or everything as decimals. Often one route is much quicker than the other.

**Class examples:** 1/2 × 0.7 is easier as decimals: 0.5 × 0.7 = 0.35. 2.5 × 1⅗ is easier as fractions: 5/2 × 8/5 = 4.
