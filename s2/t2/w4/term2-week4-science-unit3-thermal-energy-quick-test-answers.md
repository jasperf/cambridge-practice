# Thermal Energy Quick Test — Answers

**Q1** | **Heat** is the transfer of thermal energy between objects because of a temperature difference, measured in joules (J). **Temperature** measures how hot or cold something is (the average kinetic energy of its particles), measured in °C.

**Q2** | The 500 mL cup holds more heat. Both have the same temperature (same average particle energy), but it has twice as many particles, so its total thermal energy is greater.

**Q3** | **B** — heat flows from the hotter object to the cooler one.

**Q4** | Conduction (spoon warming in hot soup), convection (water circulating in a heated pan, or a radiator warming a room), radiation (warmth of a campfire or the Sun).

**Q5** | Water at the bottom is heated, becomes **less dense** and **rises**. At the top it cools, becomes denser and **sinks** to take the place of the rising water. This repeats and forms a loop (convection current).

**Q6** | **D** — radiation travels as infrared waves and needs no particles, so it works through a vacuum.

**Q7** | Conduction. Metal is a good conductor, so vibrating particles pass energy along the spoon quickly. Wood is a poor conductor (an insulator), so heat moves along it slowly.

**Q8** | The trapped layer of air or gas between the two panes is a poor conductor, so less heat escapes through the window.

**Q9** | **C** — trapped air is a poor conductor and the lid stops heat escaping by convection and evaporation.

**Q10** | No cold flows. **Heat** flows from the warmer hand to the colder ice because of the difference in **temperature**. The hand's **particles** have more energy; they pass it to the ice particles, so the hand feels cold and the ice melts.
