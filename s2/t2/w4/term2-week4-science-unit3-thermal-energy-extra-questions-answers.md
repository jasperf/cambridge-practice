# Thermal Energy Extra Questions — Answers

**Q1** | Any three: it saves energy (energy efficiency), it lowers heating bills (less work for the heating system), and it protects the environment (less energy used means fewer greenhouse-gas emissions).

**Q2** | **C** — heat is measured in joules (J) or calories (cal); temperature in °C, °F or kelvin (K).

**Q3** | Wool traps air between its fibres. Trapped air is a poor conductor, so wool is an **insulator**. It slows the **heat loss** from your body to the cooler air, so you stay warm.

**Q4** | Any two: fibreglass, foam or wool in the walls and attic (loft); double-glazed windows (two panes with an air or gas gap); layers of insulating material under the floor or roof.

**Q5** | **D** — better insulation means the heating works less, so less energy is used and fewer greenhouse gases are released.

**Q6** | Any two with a reason: add fibreglass or foam insulation to the walls and attic (traps air, less heat escapes by conduction); fit double-glazed windows (the air or gas gap reduces heat loss through the glass); put on layers or close curtains (traps a layer of warm air). Less heat lost means the heating works less, so bills fall.
