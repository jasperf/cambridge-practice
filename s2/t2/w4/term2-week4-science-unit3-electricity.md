## Section A: Circuit Diagrams and Symbols

**Key ideas:** Circuit diagrams use standard **symbols**, which make complex circuits easier to interpret. ## Section B: Voltage, Current and Resistance

**Key ideas:** **Voltage** is linked to the electrical energy in a circuit and is measured in **volts (V)**. **Current** is measured in **amperes (A)** using an ammeter connected **in series**. A **resistor** limits (resists) the current flow and is measured in **ohms (Ω)**. A voltmeter is connected **in parallel** across the component it measures.

## Section C: Ohm's Law: V = I × R

**Key ideas:** **V = I × R**, so **I = V ÷ R** and **R = V ÷ I** (cover the quantity you want in the formula triangle). V is in volts, I in amperes and R in ohms. More resistance means less current for the same voltage.

## Section D: Variable Resistors and Resistor Data

**Key ideas:** A **fixed resistor** gives a fixed amount of resistance. A **variable resistor** lets you change the resistance without replacing the component, so it can vary the current and the brightness of a lamp. To find the resistance from measured data, divide each voltage by its current: **R = V ÷ I**. For a fixed resistor the answers should be close to the same value.

## Section E: Series and Parallel Circuits

**Key ideas:** **Series circuit**: one linear pathway, the **current is the same** everywhere, the **voltage divides** between components, less reliable (if one bulb fails they all go out — e.g. holiday lights). **Parallel circuit**: multiple pathways, the **voltage is the same** across each branch, the **current divides**, more reliable (used for house wiring).
