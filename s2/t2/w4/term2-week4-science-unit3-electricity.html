Circuit Diagrams and Symbols

Circuit diagrams use standard symbols, which make complex circuits easier to interpret. A cell (or battery) stores energy and provides the energy the circuit needs; a switch, when closed, lets electricity flow round the whole circuit; a lamp lights up when current flows; an ammeter measures the current; a voltmeter measures voltage; a buzzer turns electricity into sound; wires are the electrical connections.

Section B

Voltage, Current and Resistance

Voltage is linked to the electrical energy in a circuit and is measured in volts (V). Current is measured in amperes (A) using an ammeter connected in series. A resistor limits (resists) the current flow and is measured in ohms (Ω). A voltmeter is connected in parallel across the component it measures.

Volts vs amps: think of a water pipe. Voltage is like the pressure from a pump — the push the cell gives to the charge. Current is like how much water passes a point each second — the flow of charge. Small currents are often given in milliamperes: 1000 mA = 1 A.

Section C

Ohm's Law: V = I × R

V = I × R, so I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I (cover the quantity you want in the formula triangle). V is in volts, I in amperes and R in ohms. More resistance means less current for the same voltage.

Section D

Variable Resistors and Resistor Data

A fixed resistor gives a fixed amount of resistance. A variable resistor lets you change the resistance without replacing the component, so it can vary the current and the brightness of a lamp. To find the resistance from measured data, divide each voltage by its current: R = V ÷ I. For a fixed resistor the answers should be close to the same value.

Section E

Series and Parallel Circuits

Series circuit: one linear pathway, the current is the same everywhere, the voltage divides between components, less reliable (if one bulb fails they all go out — e.g. holiday lights). Parallel circuit: multiple pathways, the voltage is the same across each branch, the current divides, more reliable (used for house wiring).
