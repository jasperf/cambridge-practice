Section A

What Is Density?

Density tells us how tightly packed the particles are in a material. It depends on the mass of the substance and how much space it occupies (its volume): ρ = m ÷ V. Generally solids are the most dense, liquids less dense and gases the least, but there are exceptions.

Section C

States of Matter and Temperature

Density changes when the state or temperature changes. When a substance is heated, its particles gain energy and move more, taking up more space, so its density decreases. When it is cooled, the particles slow down and take up less space, so its density increases. Not every solid is denser than every liquid: wood is a solid but is less dense than water.

Float or Sink?

Knowing the density of a substance helps decide whether it floats or sinks in another substance. An object less dense than the liquid around it will float; an object denser than the liquid will sink. Compare the two densities — mass alone does not decide it.

Section E

Water's Anomaly — Why Ice Floats

Water is unusual because it is less dense as a solid than as a liquid. In a water molecule the hydrogen atoms are slightly positive (δ+) and the oxygen is slightly negative (δ−). In ice, each molecule forms hydrogen bonds with four others, which pushes the molecules further apart than in liquid water and gives a hexagonal structure. This is why ice cubes and icebergs float. Densities: ice 0.9168, liquid water 0.9971, water vapour 0.000588 g/cm3.
