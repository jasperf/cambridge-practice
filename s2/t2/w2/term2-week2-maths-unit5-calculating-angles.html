Section A · Lines & Points

Angle Facts

Why should I care? An angle measures a turn, and builders, pilots and players use angles all the time.
  • Carpentry: each corner of a picture frame joins two pieces cut at 45°, so together they make a 90° corner.
  • Navigation: pilots and sailors give directions as angles. Turn through 360° and you face the way you started.
  • Sport: a snooker ball bounces off the cushion at the same angle it hit it. Players use this to plan their shots.
  • Nature: bees build six-sided cells because three of them fit round a point with no gaps. You will work out why in Section D.
Three facts about angles at a point:
  • Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.
  • Angles around a point add up to 360° (a full turn).
  • When two straight lines cross, the angles opposite each other across the X are vertically opposite, and they are always the same size.
Class example: three angles meet at a point: 110°, a right angle and x. So 110 + 90 + x = 360, which gives 200 + x = 360 and x = 160°.
Always give a reason. In a test, write the angle fact next to each step, for example: x = 160° (angles around a point add up to 360°).

Triangles

Angles in a triangle add up to 180°. Tear the three corners off a paper triangle and put them side by side: they always fill a straight line. Try it in the explorer below. A quadrilateral splits into two triangles, so its angles add up to 2 × 180 = 360°.
Exterior angle of a triangle. Extend one side past a corner. The angle outside the triangle, between the extended side and the next side, is an exterior angle. It equals the two interior angles at the other two corners added together.
Class example 1: a triangle has angles of 35° and 27° at two corners. The exterior angle at the third corner is 35 + 27 = 62°. Check: the interior angle there is 180 − 62 = 118°, and 35 + 27 + 118 = 180°.
Class example 2: the base of a triangle is extended at both ends, making exterior angles of 120° and 155°. The interior angles at the base are 180 − 120 = 60° and 180 − 155 = 25°, so the top angle is 180 − 60 − 25 = 95°.
Isosceles triangles have two equal sides (marked with small ticks) and two equal angles, at the ends of the third side.

Parallel Lines

Parallel lines never meet. Diagrams mark them with matching arrows. A line that crosses them is called a transversal. It makes eight angles, but they come in only two sizes, and the two sizes add up to 180°.
NameLook forRule
Correspondingan F shape: the same position at each crossingequal
Alternatea Z shape: between the lines, on opposite sides of the transversalequal
Co-interiora C (or U) shape: between the lines, on the same sidetogether make 180°
Class examples (Workbook page 66): a = 80° (corresponding angles), b = 80° (vertically opposite), c = 55° (corresponding angles), d = 180 − 55 = 125° (angles on a straight line). Every step has its reason written next to it.

Section D · Unit 5.2

Interior & Exterior Angles of Polygons

A polygon is a flat, closed shape with straight sides. Names to know: pentagon (5 sides), hexagon (6), heptagon (7), octagon (8), nonagon (9), decagon (10).
PolygonSidesTriangles from one cornerSum of interior angles
Triangle311 × 180 = 180°
Quadrilateral422 × 180 = 360°
Pentagon533 × 180 = 540°
n sidesnn − 2180(n − 2)°
Why n − 2? Draw every diagonal from one corner. The two sides that touch that corner are already sides of the first and last triangle, so an n-sided polygon splits into n − 2 triangles.
Exterior angles always add up to 360°. Walk all the way round the shape. At each corner you turn through the exterior angle, and when you are back at the start you have made exactly one full turn. At each corner, interior + exterior = 180°.
Regular polygons have all sides equal and all angles equal. Each exterior angle is 360 ÷ n, and each interior angle is 180° minus the exterior angle.
Class example: a pentagon has angles a, 160°, 50°, 140° and 90°. The sum is 180 × (5 − 2) = 540°, so a + 440 = 540 and a = 100°.

Section E · Putting It Together

Algebra with Angles

Angles written with letters. Three steps:
  1. Pick the angle fact: straight line, triangle, polygon sum, parallel lines, and so on.
  2. Write an equation and solve it.
  3. Put the value back in to find each angle, then check the total.
Class example 1: a quadrilateral has angles 3x + 80, 5x + 10, 3x − 20 and 4x − 10. They add up to 360°, so 15x + 60 = 360, 15x = 300 and x = 20. The angles are 140°, 110°, 40° and 70°.
Class example 2: co-interior angles 8x + 15 and 3x add up to 180°, so 11x + 15 = 180, 11x = 165 and x = 15.
