# Secondary 2 · Semester 1 — UN Day Writing Project: *Journey into the Past*

A coaching guide for the handwritten UN Day assignment (English, 50 marks). It plans the work and explains how to score well, but the writing itself must be the student's own.

- **Task:** a historical narrative or descriptive recount about a significant event, landmark or cultural tradition from the past of the class's assigned country (**Zimbabwe** or **Sweden**)
- **Length:** 300–350 words, handwritten
- **Display:** the final piece is mounted for the UN Day classroom gallery walk
- **Assigned country in this household:** Zimbabwe

## Choosing a topic (Zimbabwe)

A narrow topic scores better than a broad one because it leaves room for depth.

- **Great Zimbabwe** (strongest choice): the stone city built roughly between the 11th and 15th centuries by the ancestors of the Shona, a trading centre, the origin of the country's name (usually explained as "houses of stone"), the home of the Zimbabwe Bird carvings, and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
- **Independence, 18 April 1980:** a story about people and events.
- **The Mutapa Kingdom**, **mbira music** or **Shona stone sculpture** as alternatives.

Check every date and fact against **at least two reliable sources** (UNESCO, a museum, an encyclopedia). Sources sometimes disagree on dates, and the "Historical Accuracy & Content" criterion rewards getting this right.

## Draft outline (about 330 words)

1. **Hook (~40 words):** open with a vivid scene, such as standing before the walls.
2. **Background (~90 words):** who, when and why.
3. **The heart (~120 words):** the key event or feature, with specific details and numbers.
4. **Legacy (~70 words):** why it matters today and what it means to the people of Zimbabwe.

## Component A: how to make the edits count

The rubric rewards edits that are **visible** and **purposeful**. Aim for at least 3–4 edits in *every* paragraph, spread across four types. Cross out and rewrite instead of erasing, because the second pen colour is the evidence.

| Edit type | Examples |
|-----------|----------|
| **Vocabulary** | big → *imposing*, old → *ancient*, got → *acquired*, showed → *demonstrated* |
| **Grammar & accuracy** | tense slips, spelling, punctuation, capital letters, facts |
| **Structure** | combine choppy sentences, reorder awkward ones, add connectives (*however*, *as a result*) |
| **Register** | "a lot of" → *numerous*, "kind of" → *somewhat*; keep a formal exhibition tone |

### Component A rubric at a glance (5 × 5 = 25)

| Criterion | Top band looks like |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Draft completeness | Full 300–350 words, fully addresses the prompt |
| Evidence of editing | Extensive visible edits throughout, in a contrasting colour |
| Language & vocabulary edits | Conscious swaps of weak words for precise, high-impact ones |
| Grammar & accuracy edits | Spelling, punctuation and structural errors meticulously corrected |
| Structural refinement | Clear restructuring of sentences to express complex ideas fluently |

## Component B: the display piece

- A **bold title**, neat margins and a decorative border. Shona-inspired patterns (chevrons, zigzags) suit Great Zimbabwe, and a small drawing of the Zimbabwe Bird is an optional extra.
- Pencil faint guidelines first, then erase them once the ink is dry. Practise the layout on scrap paper before using the good sheet.
- Fit the text in comfortably so it is readable from a short distance.

### Component B rubric at a glance (5 × 5 = 25)

| Criterion | Top band looks like |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Language & impact | Masterful word choice that evokes a vivid response |
| Historical accuracy & content | Rich, accurate and fascinating insight into the country's past |
| Display aesthetics & presentation | Clean layout, creative styling, crisp margins, strong visual effort |
| Grammar & register | Flawless grammar and spelling; formality suits an official cultural exhibition |
| Handwriting & legibility | Beautiful, fluent, legible handwriting sustained across the whole page |
