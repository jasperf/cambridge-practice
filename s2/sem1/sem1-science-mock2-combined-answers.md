## Section A: Atoms & the Periodic Table

**Q1** (1 mark): 40 − 20 = 20 neutrons

**Q2** (1 mark): 2,8,5: 2 + 8 = 10 in the first two shells, leaving 5 in the third.

**Q3** (1 mark): The atom has 8 electrons; the ion gains 2 more, so 10 electrons — a full 2,8 shell.

**Q4** (2 marks): **C**: Potassium — Group 1, Period 4 Potassium loses its single outer electron most easily: it is in Group 1, one period below sodium, so its outer electron is further from the nucleus and leaves more readily.

## Section B: Bonding & Chemical Structures

**Q5** (1 mark): Lithium bromide (LiBr).

**Q6** (1 mark): **A**: Sodium chloride (NaCl) Sodium is a metal and chlorine a non-metal, so electrons are transferred and the opposite charges attract. The others are covalent molecules.

**Q7** (1 mark): 2 bonds × 2 pairs = 4 shared pairs of electrons.

**Q8** (1 mark): Al₂O₃: two 3+ ions give 6+; three 2− ions give 6−.

**Q9** (2 marks): **D**: Simple molecules Simple molecules have weak forces between the molecules, so they boil low. Giant structures (covalent, ionic or metallic) all have strong bonds throughout and high melting points.

**Q10** (2 marks): **B**: The bonds inside each sheet are strong, but the forces holding one sheet to the next are weak, so the sheets slide. Each sheet is a strong network of covalent bonds, which is why the sheets stay whole. What gives way is the weak attraction between sheets, so they slide over each other.

## Section C: Photosynthesis & Minerals

**Q11** (1 mark): Starch.

**Q12** (1 mark): **D**: Carbon dioxide Plants get their carbon from carbon dioxide in the air, through the leaves, not from the soil. The mineral ions supply nitrogen, magnesium and potassium.

**Q13** (1 mark): **B**: The leaf contains starch Iodine turns blue-black with starch, so the leaf has been photosynthesising and storing the glucose as starch.

**Q14** (1 mark): **C**: Without light the pondweed cannot photosynthesise, so it makes no oxygen The bubbles are oxygen made by photosynthesis. Light is the energy source, so in the dark no photosynthesis happens and no oxygen is made.

**Q15** (1 mark): 36 ÷ 4 = 9 bubbles per minute

**Q16** (2 marks): 7.5 − 6.0 = 1.5. 1.5 ÷ 6.0 × 100 = 25%

## Section D: Transport, Transpiration & the Carbon Cycle

**Q17** (2 marks): **B**: Phloem Phloem carries dissolved sugar up and down, from stores to wherever it is needed. Xylem only carries water and minerals upward.

**Q18** (1 mark): Transpiration.

**Q19** (2 marks): **A**: With no light there is no photosynthesis, so open stomata would only lose water In daylight the plant needs carbon dioxide for photosynthesis, so losing water through open stomata is worth it. In the dark there is nothing to gain, so closing the stomata saves water.

**Q20** (1 mark): Respiration: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water.

**Q21** (1 mark): 9000 − 3000 = 6000 tonnes are added to the air.

## Section F: Density

**Q28** (2 marks): V = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 cm³, so ρ = 90 ÷ 60 = 1.5 g/cm³.

**Q29** (1 mark): 75 − 60 = 15 mL, so the volume is 15 cm³.

**Q30** (1 mark): 2.7 × 1000 = 2700 kg/m³

**Q31** (1 mark): **A**: It floats 7.9 g/cm³ is less than 13.6 g/cm³, so the iron floats, even though it would sink in water.

**Q32** (2 marks): **D**: The warm air expands, becomes less dense than the cool air around it, and cooler air sinks beneath it and pushes it up The mass stays the same but the volume grows, so the density falls. Cool, denser air sinks underneath and lifts the warm air — that is convection.

## Section G: Sound & Waves

**Q33** (1 mark): f = 1 ÷ 0.005 = 200 Hz

**Q34** (1 mark): **A**: Increase the amplitude of the vibration A bigger amplitude carries more energy, so the note is louder. Frequency decides pitch, so it must stay the same.

**Q35** (1 mark): **B**: Light from the Sun Light vibrates at right angles to the direction it travels. Sound — and its slinky model — is longitudinal.

**Q36** (1 mark): 3 cm up meets 3 cm down: they cancel exactly, so the amplitude is 0.

## Section H: Thermal Energy & Conservation

**Q37** (2 marks): **D**: Metal conducts heat away from your body faster than wood does, so your nerves sense a bigger heat loss Conduction: your body loses heat to the metal much faster than to the wood, even at the same temperature. Wood is a poor conductor, so it feels warmer.

**Q38** (1 mark): **C**: Radiation Infrared radiation travels through empty space. Conduction and convection both need particles; evaporation is not a way for heat to cross a vacuum.

**Q39** (1 mark): Evaporation: the fastest particles escape as vapour, taking energy with them, so what is left behind cools.

**Q40** (2 marks): 800 − 500 − 240 = 60 J of sound.

**Q41** (2 marks): 1600 ÷ 2000 × 100 = 80%

## Section I: Electricity

**Q42** (2 marks): R = 3 + 9 = 12 Ω, so I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 12 = 1 A.

**Q43** (1 mark): R = V ÷ I = 5 ÷ 0.25 = 20 Ω

**Q44** (1 mark): 0.4 + 0.4 = 0.8 A

**Q45** (1 mark): **A**: It goes out too — the loop is broken and no current can flow A series circuit is one single loop. A break anywhere stops the current everywhere — which is one reason houses are wired in parallel.

**Q46** (1 mark): **C**: An ammeter The ammeter measures the current flowing through it, so it sits in series on the wire. The voltmeter connects across a component, in parallel.
