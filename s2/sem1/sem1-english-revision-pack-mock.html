Section A · Worksheet 1 · Q1–Q7

Reading Comprehension

Inference means working out what the writer suggests without saying it. Back each idea with a short quotation, and use the surrounding words to work out what an unfamiliar word means.
Text A: The Last Bus

[1] Ira had been waiting at the stop for forty minutes, and the timetable pinned behind its scratched plastic had given up pretending. The sky was the colour of wet slate. Every few minutes she checked her phone, though she knew it would say nothing new.

[2] “You’ll catch your death,” said an old man on the bench, not looking up from his newspaper. He wore a coat two sizes too big and held a flask in both hands like a candle.

[3] “I’m fine,” Ira said, which was a lie her teeth contradicted at once.

[4] The man unscrewed the flask and, without a word, poured something steaming into its lid and held it out. She hesitated. Then the smell of ginger reached her, and her fingers closed round the warm metal before her pride could object.

[5] When the bus finally lurched round the corner, its headlights swung over them both. The man folded his newspaper, tucked it under his arm and stood up slowly. “Plenty of room,” he said. “Nobody comes this far out any more.”

[6] Ira climbed on behind him. For the first time that evening she was no longer counting the minutes.

Section B · Worksheet 2 · Q8–Q14

Text Analysis

Purpose is why a text was written; audience is who it is for; tone is the attitude in the voice; viewpoint is the writer’s opinion. Ask how each choice of words and structure serves them.
Text B: Blog post, “Let Us Sleep!”

Be honest: who actually enjoys a 7:15 start? I’m fifteen, and my alarm goes off in the dark every single morning. By second period my brain is still in bed. Scientists say teenagers need around nine hours of sleep; most of my class gets six.

The plan to start school at 9:00 is the best idea in years. Some people say it will mess up the bus timetables. Honestly? Buses can change. Our brains can’t. So, head teacher, be brave: let us sleep!

Text C: Letter to a local newspaper

Dear Editor,

I write as the parent of two teenagers and as a bus driver of twenty years. I understand the wish for more sleep. However, a 9:00 start would push the school day to nearly 4:30, leaving little daylight for sport and sending many pupils home in the dark.

The council has published no plan for after-school transport. I would urge the school to run a three-month trial before any final decision is made.

Yours faithfully,
A. Hadi

Section C · Worksheet 3 · Q15–Q20

Summary Writing

A good summary keeps only the main points, uses your own words, adds no opinion, and stays inside the word limit. Examples, names and numbers are usually details to leave out.
Text D: Article, “Small Libraries, Big Ideas”

[1] Across the country, tiny free libraries made from old cupboards and wooden boxes are appearing on street corners. Anyone can take a book or leave one, and nobody needs a library card. Volunteers usually build and look after them.

[2] Supporters say the boxes help people who live far from a real library, and many neighbours begin chatting while they browse. One organiser, Tomas, said that four children in his street now read every day.

[3] However, the boxes have problems. Rain can ruin the books, and some stand empty for weeks when nobody donates. A few have been damaged by vandals. Libraries say the boxes are welcome but cannot replace trained staff.

Q17
