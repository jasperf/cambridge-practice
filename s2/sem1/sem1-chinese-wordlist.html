How to use these cards to memorize

Read once — then do it every time
Short answer: do not read the list. To memorize characters you test yourself (your brain only keeps what it has to work for), you write them, and you come back over several days. The word list above is your deck — the routine below turns it into memory. Reveal all / Hide all are for a final check, never for learning.
1

Shuffle first

Press Shuffle in the part you are doing, so you learn the words and not the order of the list.

2

Say it, then picture it

In Part A say the Chinese word out loud with its tones, then picture or air-write the characters before you tap. In Part B read the characters and say the English meaning.

3

Tap to check — honestly

Only tap after you have given an answer. If you peeked, tap the box to hide it again and add that word to your again pile.

4

Write every word you missed

Copy a missed word 3 times on paper, saying it as you write. The exam asks you to write characters, so the hand has to know them as well as the eyes.

5

Do the wrong pile again

Hide the words you got wrong and loop until one full pass has zero wrong. Do both parts: writing a word from English (Part A) is harder than recognising it (Part B).

6

Short sessions, repeated

10–15 minutes a day beats one long cram. Words stick when you return to them tomorrow.

Golden rule: never reveal a card before you have tried it. A card you peek at is a card you have not learned.

Memory tricks that work with Chinese

Break characters into parts. 律师 (lawyer) and 医生 (doctor) both start with a person-like part; 鞋 (shoe) has the leather radical 革 on the left. Spot the repeated parts — 皮鞋, 高跟鞋 and 运动鞋 all end in 鞋, so you only learn it once.
Learn measure words in pairs. 一双鞋 (a pair of shoes), 一件外套 (a coat). Say the whole phrase every time, not the measure word alone.
Say it in a sentence. 夏天很热 (summer is hot), 我妈妈是经理 (my mum is a manager), 这双鞋太贵了 (these shoes are too expensive). A word in your own sentence turns “I have seen this” into “I can use this”.
Use the pairs. 因为……所以…… and 虽然……但是…… always come as a pair. Learn the frame, then change what goes inside it.
Tones matter. The pinyin tells you the tone: 买 (mǎi, buy) and 卖 (mài, sell) differ only in tone. Say tones out loud when you check.
Sleep on it. Do your last pass in the evening. Sleep files new words into long-term memory, so a midnight cram does little.
