## Section A: From Atoms to Ions

**Q1** (1 mark): 20 − 2 = 18 electrons (2,8,8). It still has 20 protons.

**Q2** (2 marks): Sulfur is 2,8,6. It gains two electrons, so S²⁻ is 2,8,8: a full outer shell, like argon.

**Q3** (1 mark): **B**: A magnesium ion, Mg²⁺ 12 protons makes it magnesium. Two fewer electrons than protons means a charge of 2+. (Neon and O²⁻ also have 10 electrons, but not 12 protons.)

**Q4** (1 mark): 5 + 3 = 8, so it gains 3 electrons and becomes N³⁻ (a nitride ion).

**Q5** (1 mark): Fluoride (F⁻)

**Q6** (1 mark): **D**: Al³⁺ and F⁻ Al (13) loses 3 → 10 electrons; F (9) gains 1 → 10 electrons. Both are 2,8. K⁺ and Cl⁻ are 2,8,8; Li⁺ and Be²⁺ are 2; S²⁻ is 2,8,8.

**Q7** (1 mark): **A**: It loses one electron and still has 19 protons. Only electrons move. Losing one negative electron leaves one more proton than electrons, so the charge is 1+. The 19 protons keep it potassium.

**Q8** (2 marks): 10 − 3 = 7 electrons in the atom, so 7 protons: X is nitrogen.

## Section B: Writing Ionic Formulae

**Q9** (1 mark): Li₂S: two Li⁺ (2+) balance one S²⁻ (2−).

**Q10** (1 mark): AlF₃: one Al³⁺ (3+) balances three F⁻ (3−).

**Q11** (2 marks): Ca₃N₂: three Ca²⁺ give 6+, two N³⁻ give 6−.

**Q12** (2 marks): Al₂O₃: two Al³⁺ give 6+, three O²⁻ give 6−.

**Q13** (1 mark): Na⁺ and O²⁻, so two Na⁺ balance one O²⁻: Na₂O.

**Q14** (1 mark): **C**: Calcium bromide Calcium bromide: the metal keeps its name and bromine becomes bromide. The “2” is not said in the name of an ionic compound.

**Q15** (1 mark): **A**: The charges do not balance: one Mg²⁺ needs two F⁻, so it is MgF₂. Mg²⁺ + F⁻ leaves 1+ left over. Two F⁻ ions are needed: MgF₂.

**Q16** (1 mark): 2+: two Cl⁻ give 2−, so the single barium ion must be Ba²⁺. (Barium is in Group 2.)

## Section C: Ionic Compounds

**Q17** (1 mark): Ionic: positive and negative ions alternate in a regular lattice, held by attraction between opposite charges.

**Q18** (1 mark): **D**: Melts at 714 °C; conducts only when melted or dissolved A high melting point plus conducting only when molten or dissolved is the pattern of an ionic compound. B is a metal (copper), A is a covalent molecule (bromine).

**Q19** (2 marks): **B**: Millions of strong attractions between Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions all have to be overcome. There are no NaCl molecules. Each ion is pulled on from every side by oppositely charged ions, and all of those attractions must be overcome to melt the lattice.

**Q20** (1 mark): Ions (Cu²⁺ and Cl⁻), which can move once dissolved. It is not electrons that flow through the solution.

**Q21** (1 mark): **C**: When a layer shifts, ions with the same charge end up side by side and repel. Sliding one layer puts + next to + and − next to −. These repel, so the crystal splits apart. A metal has no such problem, because its sea of electrons keeps holding the layers.

**Q22** (1 mark): **D**: Towards the negative electrode K⁺ is positive, so it is attracted to the negative electrode. The Br⁻ ions move the other way, to the positive electrode.

## Section D: Covalent Bonding

**Q23** (1 mark): Nitrogen is 3 short, so it makes 3 bonds, one to each of 3 hydrogen atoms: NH₃.

**Q24** (1 mark): CCl₄: four chlorine atoms, one on each of carbon’s four bonds.

**Q25** (2 marks): 4 bonds × 2 electrons each = 8 shared electrons.

**Q26** (1 mark): 6 of its own unshared electrons + the 2 in the shared pair = 8: a full outer shell.

**Q27** (2 marks): **D**: Boiling only separates whole water molecules, and the forces between them are weak. Steam is still H₂O, so no covalent bonds break. Only the weak forces between molecules are overcome, which takes little energy. Melting NaCl means breaking strong ionic attractions.

**Q28** (1 mark): **C**: Sulfur dioxide Sulfur and oxygen are both non-metals, so they share electrons. Potassium oxide and lithium fluoride are ionic (metal + non-metal); calcium is a metal.

**Q29** (1 mark): A triple bond (6 shared electrons). Each nitrogen was 3 short of a full shell.

**Q30** (1 mark): 2 double bonds × 2 pairs = 4 shared pairs (8 electrons). Carbon makes 4 bonds in total.

## Section E: Metallic Bonding & Telling Them Apart

**Q31** (1 mark): **B**: Metallic Metallic: identical positive ions with free electrons spread between them. In an ionic lattice the charges would alternate.

**Q32** (2 marks): **C**: Copper has electrons that are free to move; the ions in solid copper chloride are held in place. Copper’s delocalised electrons can drift through it. Copper chloride is full of ions, but in the solid they cannot move, so no current flows until it melts or dissolves.

**Q33** (1 mark): Metallic: an alloy contains only metals.

**Q34** (1 mark): **A**: Magnesium: metallic Magnesium is a metal element, so metallic. Calcium chloride is ionic (metal + non-metal); ammonia and carbon dioxide are covalent (non-metals only).

**Q35** (1 mark): Metallic (it is iron). Conducting as a solid and bending without breaking both come from the sea of electrons.

**Q36** (1 mark): Covalent: small molecules with weak forces between them, and no ions or free electrons to carry a current.
