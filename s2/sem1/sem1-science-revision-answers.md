## Section A: Atoms & the Periodic Table

**Q1** (1 mark): 39 − 19 = 20 neutrons

**Q2** (1 mark): Chlorine is 2,8,7. One extra electron gives 2,8,8: a full outer shell, like argon.

**Q3** (1 mark): Group 3: three electrons in the outer shell. (It is aluminium, in Period 3.)

**Q4** (2 marks): **C**: Fluorine’s outer shell is closest to the nucleus, so an incoming electron is attracted most strongly. Going down Group 7 each atom has one more shell, so the outer shell is further from the nucleus and pulls less strongly on an extra electron. Fluorine, at the top, pulls hardest.

**Q5** (1 mark): 15 protons + 16 neutrons = 31 (it is phosphorus).

**Q6** (1 mark): **A**: 2,8,8 2,8,8 is argon, a noble gas. Its outer shell is already full.

## Section B: Bonding & Ions

**Q7** (1 mark): 3+: aluminium (2,8,3) loses its 3 outer electrons.

**Q8** (1 mark): K₂O: two 1+ ions balance one 2− ion.

**Q9** (2 marks): Mg₃N₂: three Mg²⁺ give 6+, two N³⁻ give 6−, so the charges cancel.

**Q10** (1 mark): **D**: Hydrogen and chlorine Hydrogen and chlorine are both non-metals, so they share a pair of electrons (HCl). Sodium fluoride and magnesium oxide are ionic; copper and zinc are both metals.

**Q11** (1 mark): A double bond is 2 shared pairs, so 4 electrons.

**Q12** (2 marks): **B**: In the solid the ions are locked in place; in the liquid they can move and carry charge. The ions themselves carry the current. They can only flow once the lattice breaks up on melting (or dissolving).

**Q25** (1 mark): T = 1 ÷ 50 = 0.02 s

**Q27** (1 mark): 3 + 4 = 7 cm

**Q29** (1 mark): Rarefactions

**Q30** (1 mark): **A**: The ringing gets quieter and quieter, until it can hardly be heard. As air is removed there are fewer particles to pass the vibrations on, so less sound reaches you. In a perfect vacuum there would be none.

## Section F: Thermal Energy

**Q31** (2 marks): **B**: The bath has more thermal energy: its particles move more slowly, but there are vastly more of them. The sparkler is hotter (higher average energy per particle), but the huge mass of bath water stores far more energy in total.

**Q32** (1 mark): 25 °C: once the tea matches the room, there is no more heat flow.

**Q33** (1 mark): Radiation (infrared)

**Q34** (1 mark): **C**: The thin gap of air between the panes conducts heat badly and is too narrow to circulate. Still air conducts heat very poorly, and the gap is too narrow for convection currents, so heat escapes slowly.

**Q35** (2 marks): **A**: The fastest water particles escape from your skin as vapour, taking energy with them. This is evaporation. The most energetic particles leave, so the average energy of what remains drops and your skin cools. Wind carries the vapour away, which speeds it up.

**Q36** (1 mark): Solids: the particles can only vibrate in place.

## Section G: Electricity

**Q37** (1 mark): I = V ÷ R = 6 ÷ 3 = 2 A

**Q38** (1 mark): V = I × R = 0.2 × 45 = 9 V

**Q39** (1 mark): 5 + 7 + 12 = 24 Ω

**Q40** (2 marks): I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 24 = 0.5 A

**Q41** (1 mark): **D**: In parallel, with one lead on each side of the lamp. The voltmeter goes across the lamp, in parallel. It is the ammeter that goes in series.

**Q42** (1 mark): **B**: Each light gets the full mains voltage and can be switched on or off without the others. Every parallel branch gets the full voltage, so each lamp is fully bright, and a broken or switched-off lamp does not break the circuit for the others.
