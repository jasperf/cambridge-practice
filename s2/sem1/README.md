# Secondary 2 · Semester 1 Assessment — Mock Papers

Full-length practice papers for the Semester 1 assessments, **Monday 23 – Friday 27 November 2026** (each exam finishes at 11:00). Each paper pulls together every topic taught in Term 1 and Term 2 so far, with all-new questions.

Every paper comes in three files with the same basename:

- **`.html`**: the interactive sheet. Every question is auto-marked (multiple choice, a typed number or a typed word). Diagrams draw themselves in as you scroll.
- **`.md`**: the printable paper. It has the same questions in the same order, plus **written questions** at the end that are not on the interactive sheet (explanations, P.E.E.E. paragraphs, constructions, extended writing). These are marked by the teacher.
- **`-answers.md`**: answers to every auto-marked question and a marking guide for the written questions. It is kept separate, so the question paper can be handed out on its own.

### Bahasa Indonesia

Built from the S2 Bahasa Indonesia SA revision list (Level 1), so every topic on it has questions. Section A, *Di Dalam Kelasku*: a reading text about a classroom, classroom vocabulary, prepositions of place (with a four-picture diagram), the verb *ada*, *apakah*, personal pronouns and possessive pronouns (-ku, -mu). Section B, *Teks Iklan*: two original advertisements (a food promo and an apartment) with questions on vocabulary, information, prices and times, the question words *apa*, *di mana*, *siapa* and *berapa*, and *kalimat ajakan* with *-lah* and *-kan*. Section C, *Mengenal Hewan*: animal vocabulary, body parts on a fish and a bird, adjectives and opposites, comparative sentences read from a bar chart of animal heights, *punya / mempunyai / memiliki*, and the prefix *ber-*. 29 of the 76 questions are multiple choice; the rest are typed Indonesian words with the accepted spellings checked. The printable has the same Q1–Q76 plus seven written tasks (Q77–Q83, 74 marks, marked by the teacher): write your own advertisement (30 marks, split into title, product information and persuasive sentences), describe a classroom, ask and answer with the four question words, describe an animal, comparative sentences, translation and *ber-* rewriting. The fable unit (*Fabel*) is not on the revision list and is not covered. All texts and questions are original.

**Vocabulary drills.** Two extra 40-question drills for the words behind the mock: [Drill 1](./sem1-bahasa-indonesia-vocab.html) ([printable](./sem1-bahasa-indonesia-vocab.md) · [answers](./sem1-bahasa-indonesia-vocab-answers.md)) and [Drill 2](./sem1-bahasa-indonesia-vocab-2.html) ([printable](./sem1-bahasa-indonesia-vocab-2.md) · [answers](./sem1-bahasa-indonesia-vocab-2-answers.md)). Every question is typed — 20 English → Indonesian, 20 Indonesian → English, 1 mark each, no multiple choice — grouped by classroom, advertisements, animals, prepositions and question words. The Hints describe a word without revealing it, Drill 2 repeats none of Drill 1's words, and both printables list every accepted spelling in their answer sheets. Both drills are generated with `scripts/build_vocab_drill.py` from specs in `scripts/review_specs/`.

### English Level 2/3

Section A covers the four sentence types, FANBOYS, MOTH, semicolons and comma rules (Term 1 Unit 1). Section B is a student magazine article, *Dig In: Why Every School Needs a Garden*, with questions on viewpoint, simile, juxtaposition, statistics, expert opinion, rule of three, concession, anecdote, repetition, a P.E.E.E. *Explore* step and imperatives. Section C is a parent’s reply, *Gardens Can Wait*, with questions on claim, evidence and transitions (Term 1 Unit Test 2), followed by questions comparing the two texts. Section D applies persuasive and emotive techniques to new sentences (Term 2 Unit Test 1). The printable adds a P.E.E.E. paragraph, a comparison of how the two writers use concession, sentence rewriting and a 300-word persuasive speech or letter (20 marks). Both texts are original. Only the Level 2/3 paper is written; the Level 1 format (three shorter papers) is not covered here.

### English Revision Pack

A second English paper built from the ten-worksheet scope in the Stage 9 English Revision Pack (Units 9.1–9.3), so every topic on that list has its own section: A reading comprehension (inference, evidence and vocabulary from context, on an original story extract), B text analysis (purpose, audience, tone and viewpoint across a blog post and a letter), C summary writing (main points against details, own words, no opinion), D punctuation (colons, semi-colons, brackets, dashes, ellipses, dialogue and comma splices), E sentence structures (simple, compound, complex and compound-complex, minor sentences and clause order), F connectives (purpose and linking paragraphs), G figurative language and sound devices (simile, metaphor, personification, alliteration), H persuasive techniques (including antithesis), I argumentative writing (fact against opinion, paragraph order, counter-argument and conclusion) and J organisation and accuracy (register, commonly confused words, linking and proofreading). 43 of the 69 questions are multiple choice (the correct letter is spread across A–D); the other 26 are typed words. Every question on the interactive sheet is auto-marked. The printable `.md` has the same Q1–Q69 plus 22 written questions (Q70–Q91, 125 marks, marked by the teacher): inference paragraphs, a text comparison, two summaries, punctuating a passage, a paragraph using all four sentence types, rewriting with connectives, descriptive writing with four devices, persuasive rewriting, a planning table, a counter-argument paragraph, a 350–450 word article or speech, a formal email and a proofreading task. The texts and questions are original, and the earlier English Level 2/3 mock above covers related topics with different questions.

### Mathematics

The two papers follow the Cambridge Lower Secondary Checkpoint maths format (0862): **Paper 1 is non-calculator and Paper 2 allows a calculator. Each is 1 hour long, worth 50 marks, and mixes every unit.** So both papers test Unit 1 (rational and irrational numbers, indices, standard form), Unit 2 (expressions and formulae), Unit 3 (decimals, percentages, rounding and bounds), Unit 4 (simultaneous equations, inequalities), Unit 5 (angles, polygons, constructions, Pythagoras) and Unit 7.1–7.2 (circles, compound shapes). Questions that need a calculator, such as compound interest, Pythagoras with decimals and circle areas to 1 d.p., are in Paper 2. Paper 1 uses equivalent calculations, answers in terms of π and π ≈ 3. Each paper has a Checkpoint-style “tick every true statement” question, which scores only if every tick is right. Diagrams draw themselves in as you scroll: number lines, an area model, a lift loaded with boxes, a pair of lines crossing on a graph, parallel lines, polygons, a ladder, circles and compound shapes. They are not to scale. Paper 1’s written questions include a ruler-and-compass construction. **Paper 1B** is a second non-calculator paper for another round of practice. It has the same sections and marks as Paper 1, but every question is new (e.g. alternate angles, an isosceles triangle, a regular octagon, a rectangle with a semicircle). **Paper 1C** is a third set in the same format (e.g. corresponding angles, a regular pentagon, a quarter circle, a running-track shape).

For revision before the maths papers, the [maths formula sheets](../maths-formula-sheets/README.md) now include one-page sheets for expanding brackets, simultaneous equations, indices and standard form, inequalities, decimals and bounds, and formulae with algebraic fractions (Sheets 7–12).

### Science

Section A covers atomic structure, electronic structure, the periodic table and ionic, covalent and metallic bonding. Section B covers photosynthesis, chlorophyll, uses of glucose, magnesium and nitrate, and the pondweed and starch-test investigations. Sections C–E cover density (including displacement and why ice floats), waves read from a graph, sound, heat and temperature, convection and conduction, V = IR, and series and parallel circuits. The printable adds six written questions: a dot-and-cross diagram, conduction in molten salt, the pondweed data, why a steel ship floats, sound in a vacuum, and series versus parallel lamps.

### Bonding & Ions practice

A focused drill on the topic that gives the most trouble in the review: [interactive](./sem1-science-bonding-ions.html) · [printable](./sem1-science-bonding-ions.md) · [answers](./sem1-science-bonding-ions-answers.md). Five short sections build it up step by step: from atoms to ions (charges, counting electrons, naming), writing ionic formulae, ionic compounds (lattice, melting points, conducting, why crystals shatter), covalent bonding (counting bonds and shared electrons, dot-and-cross, simple molecules) and metallic bonding, ending with how to tell the three types apart from data. **36 questions, 44 marks, 14 MCQ**, all auto-marked. Three explorers: **Electron Transfer** (watch Na → Cl and Mg → O), a **Charge Balancer** with made-up ions M and X, and **Can It Conduct?** (an ionic lattice, solid versus melted). The printable adds six written questions (Q37–Q42, 19 marks): dot-and-cross for MgO and NH₃, why carbon shares instead of forming ions, explaining four conductivity results, bending metals versus shattering crystals, and identifying bonding from data.

Built from `scripts/review_specs/s2_sem1_science_bonding_ions.py` in the same way.

### Gaps drill

Five topics on the science revision list that the other sheets hardly cover: [interactive](./sem1-science-gaps.html) · [printable](./sem1-science-gaps.md) · [answers](./sem1-science-gaps-answers.md). Section A is the human excretory system (urea and the liver, kidneys, ureters, bladder and urethra, how nephrons filter and take back, water balance), with a numbered diagram to label. Section B is absorption, transport and transpiration (root hair cells, xylem and phloem, stomata, a potometer and a mass-loss reading, and the petroleum-jelly leaf experiment). Section C is the carbon cycle (photosynthesis, respiration, feeding, decomposers, fossil fuels and combustion, a burned rainforest, CO₂ since 1750 and hydrogencarbonate indicator). Section D is conservation of energy (useful and wasted energy, a Sankey diagram, and the class's heat-conservation slides). Section E is simple and giant structures (diamond, graphite and silicon dioxide). **37 questions, 49 marks, 14 MCQ**, all auto-marked. Three explorers come **after** their section's questions, so they cannot give answers away: a **Kidney Filter** (blood particles sorted into blood or urine, with a normal, hot or well-watered day), a **Transpiration Lab** (temperature, wind, humidity and day or night) and **Run the Carbon Cycle** (move carbon between five stores and watch the CO₂ in the air). The printable adds six written questions (Q38–Q43, 21 marks): drawing the urinary system, water's journey from soil to air, drawing a carbon cycle, planning a wind-and-transpiration investigation, a Sankey diagram for a filament bulb, and diamond versus graphite.

The class slides do not cover most of these topics, so the facts follow the Cambridge Lower Secondary Stage 9 syllabus; check them against your class notes. Built from `scripts/review_specs/s2_sem1_science_gaps.py`.
