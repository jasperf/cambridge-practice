## Section D: Punctuation

**Q21** (1 mark): *colon*: it introduces the list that follows a complete clause.

**Q22** (1 mark): *semi-colon*: it joins two closely related main clauses.

**Q23** (1 mark): **B**: A pair of colons: commas, brackets and dashes can all wrap an aside; colons introduce, they do not enclose.

**Q24** (1 mark): *ellipsis*: the dots show hesitation or trailing off.

**Q25** (1 mark): **D**: The first sentence: speech marks around each spoken part, the question mark inside the marks, and a new sentence for each speaker.

**Q26** (1 mark): **B**: The comma shows Grandma is being spoken to; without it she would be the meal. Punctuation can change meaning completely.

**Q27** (1 mark): **D**: I love swimming, I train every day: it needs a semi-colon, a full stop or a conjunction such as *so*.

**Q28** (1 mark): **C**: It adds drama by creating a sudden pause before the surprise.

## Section E: Sentence Structures

**Q29** (1 mark): **B**: Compound: two main clauses joined by *and*.

**Q30** (1 mark): **C**: Complex: one dependent clause plus one main clause.

**Q31** (1 mark): **D**: Compound-complex: a dependent clause (*Although she was tired*) plus two main clauses joined by *and*.

**Q32** (1 mark): *compound*: two main clauses joined by *or*.

**Q33** (1 mark): **B**: Unless: it introduces a dependent clause. *But*, *so* and *or* are coordinating conjunctions.

**Q34** (1 mark): **C**: The reason is now emphasised first, and a comma is needed after the dependent clause.

**Q35** (1 mark): **A**: Silence.: it has no verb but works as a sentence, adding a sudden, tense beat.

## Section F: Connectives

**Q36** (1 mark): **D**: Contrasting: *nevertheless* means “in spite of that”.

**Q37** (1 mark): **D**: Showing time or sequence: *meanwhile* places events at the same time.

**Q38** (1 mark): Any contrasting connective, e.g. *however* or *nevertheless*.

**Q39** (1 mark): Any cause-and-effect connective, e.g. *therefore*, *consequently* or *as a result*.

**Q40** (1 mark): **B**: However, shop owners worry about losing customers: the second paragraph contrasts with the benefit described in the first.

**Q41** (1 mark): **C**: *In conclusion* sums up and finishes; it does not add another point.

## Section H: Persuasive Techniques

**Q49** (1 mark): *rhetorical question*: it makes the audience agree in their heads.

**Q50** (1 mark): *imperative*: command verbs that urge the audience to act.

## Section I: Argumentative Writing

**Q56** (1 mark): *F*: it can be checked.

**Q57** (1 mark): *O*: *should* shows a view that people can disagree with.

**Q58** (1 mark): **D**: C, E, D, A, B: opening viewpoint (C), *Firstly* (E), *Secondly* (D), counter-argument and rebuttal (A), conclusion with a call to action (B).

**Q59** (1 mark): **C**: Some people believe that…: it states the opposing view without attacking those who hold it.

**Q60** (1 mark): **D**: Every bag we refuse is a turtle saved: it ends with a memorable image and a call to action.

**Q61** (1 mark): **A**: Greeting the audience: speeches begin by addressing listeners directly. Headlines and subheadings belong to articles.

## Section J: Organisation & Accuracy

**Q62** (1 mark): *consultative*: a polite question to an adviser or expert.

**Q63** (1 mark): *You’re*: the contraction of “you are”.

**Q64** (1 mark): *Whether*: “if”. *Weather* is the rain and sun.

**Q65** (1 mark): *fewer*: use it with things you can count; *less* is for quantities.

**Q66** (1 mark): **D**: Dear Mr Lee, I would be grateful if…: formal greeting, full words and polite phrasing.

**Q67** (1 mark): **A**: Despite these benefits, gardens do cost money: it refers back (“these benefits”) and signals contrast.

**Q68** (1 mark): **A**: They’re going to the museum, and their bus leaves at nine: *they’re* = they are; *their* = belonging to them.

**Q69** (1 mark): *supposed*: it has two *p*s.

- Evidence (1 each, max 2): e.g. “without a word, poured something steaming”, “Plenty of room”, “Nobody comes this far out any more”.
- Explain (1): links the quotation to the inference, e.g. he helps without making a fuss, and the last line hints that he is used to being alone.

**Q71** (4 marks) · *Section A*
