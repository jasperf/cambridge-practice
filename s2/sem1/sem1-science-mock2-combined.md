## Section A: Atoms & the Periodic Table

### Key facts

- **Inside an atom:** Protons (+1) and neutrons (no charge) in the nucleus; electrons (−1) in shells around it. In a neutral atom, electrons = protons.
- **Two numbers:** atomic number = protons · mass number = protons + neutrons
- **Filling shells:** For the first 20 elements the shells hold 2, 8, 8, 2. Phosphorus (15) is 2,8,5: 2 + 8 + 5.
- **Reading the table:** Group = electrons in the outer shell. Period = shells in use. Ions have full outer shells, so count their electrons before working out numbers.
- **Trends:** Group 1 metals lose their outer electron and are more reactive going *down*. Group 7 non-metals gain one electron and are less reactive going down.

## Section B: Bonding & Chemical Structures

### Key facts

- **Ionic bonding:** Metal + non-metal. Electrons move from metal to non-metal; the opposite charges attract. Groups 1/2/3 form 1+/2+/3+ ions; Groups 5/6/7 form 3−/2−/1−.
- **Naming ionic compounds:** Metal first, then the non-metal with its name changed: *lithium bromide*, *calcium chloride*.
- **Writing formulae:** Total + = total −. Al³⁺ and O²⁻ give Al₂O₃; Li⁺ and Br⁻ give LiBr.
- **Covalent bonding:** Non-metal + non-metal share pairs of electrons. A single bond = 1 pair, a double bond = 2 pairs. In CO₂ the carbon makes two double bonds.
- **Metallic bonding:** Positive ions in a sea of delocalised electrons — which is what carries a current.
- **Simple vs giant:** Simple molecules (H₂O, CH₄) have weak forces *between* molecules and low melting points. Giant covalent structures (diamond, graphite, SiO₂) have strong bonds throughout, so very high melting points. Graphite has one free electron per atom and weak forces between its sheets.

## Section D: Transport, Transpiration & the Carbon Cycle

### Key facts

- **Xylem:** Hollow tubes of dead cells carrying water and minerals up from the roots.
- **Phloem:** Tubes of living cells carrying dissolved sugar from the leaves to wherever it is needed, up or down.
- **Transpiration:** Water evaporates inside the leaf and leaves as vapour through the *stomata*. Root hairs take water in by osmosis; the xylem stream pulls more water up.
- **Stomata:** Opened and closed by guard cells. Faster water loss when it is hotter, windier, drier and brighter. Many close in the dark.
- **Carbon cycle:** Photosynthesis takes carbon dioxide *out* of the air. Respiration, decomposition (by bacteria and fungi) and combustion put it *back*. Feeding passes carbon along food chains; fossil fuels store it.

## Section E: Human Biology: Excretion & Inheritance

### Key facts

- **Excretion:** Removing waste made by the body’s own cells. The lungs excrete CO₂; the **liver** turns spare amino acids into **urea**; the kidneys put urea into urine.
- **Urinary system:** Kidneys filter blood (renal artery in, renal vein out) → each kidney’s urine goes down a *ureter* to the *bladder*, out through the *urethra*.
- **Nephrons:** Filter everything except cells and protein, then take back *all the glucose*, most water and the salts the body needs.
- **DNA, genes, chromosomes:** DNA is the twisted-ladder molecule; a gene is a section that codes for one feature; genes sit on chromosomes in the nucleus.
- **Inheritance:** Body cells have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs). *Meiosis* makes gametes with 23 each. Egg always carries X; sperm carries X or Y, so the sperm decides the sex: XX girl, XY boy — a 50/50 chance.

## Section F: Density

### Key facts

- **The formula:** ρ = m ÷ V, rearranged to m = ρ × V and V = m ÷ ρ.
- **Volumes:** Regular block: l × w × h. Odd object: drop it in a measuring cylinder; the rise in mL is its volume in cm³ (1 mL = 1 cm³).
- **Units:** 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³. Water is 1 g/cm³.
- **Float or sink:** Less dense than the liquid: floats. Denser: sinks. A solid can float on a denser liquid (iron floats on mercury).
- **Heating:** Particles spread out: mass unchanged, volume bigger, so density falls — which is why warm air rises.

## Section G: Sound & Waves

### Key facts

- **Two kinds of wave:** Transverse: vibrations at right angles to travel (light, water ripples). Longitudinal: vibrations along the direction of travel (sound: compressions and rarefactions).
- **Measurements:** Amplitude: middle line to a crest. Wavelength: crest to crest. Frequency: waves per second, in Hz.
- **Period:** T = 1 ÷ f. A wave with period 0.005 s has f = 1 ÷ 0.005 = 200 Hz.
- **Loud and quiet:** Bigger amplitude = louder. Higher frequency = higher pitch.
- **Superposition:** Crest meets crest: add. Crest meets trough: subtract or cancel.

## Section H: Thermal Energy & Conservation

### Key facts

- **Heat flows hot → cold:** Temperature is the average energy of the particles; thermal energy is the total and depends on mass too.
- **Three transfers:** Conduction: particle to particle, best in metals. Convection: warm liquid or gas rises, cool sinks. Radiation: infrared needs no particles, so it crosses empty space.
- **Evaporation:** The fastest particles escape from a surface at any temperature, cooling what is left behind.
- **Conservation of energy:** Energy is never created or destroyed — only transferred. energy in = useful out + wasted out, and the waste ends up heating the surroundings.
- **Sankey diagrams:** The arrow widths show the amounts. Everything out must add up to what went in.

## Section I: Electricity

### Key facts

- **Ohm’s law:** V = I × R, so I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I. Volts, amps, ohms.
- **Series:** One loop, same current everywhere, voltages add, R = R₁ + R₂. Break anywhere and everything stops.
- **Parallel:** Each branch gets the full voltage; branch currents add up. A break in one branch leaves the others working.
- **Meters:** Ammeter in *series*; voltmeter in *parallel* across the component.
