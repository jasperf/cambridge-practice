## Section A: Words to Expressions in *n*

### Key facts

- **Do the steps in order:** Each *then* works on the **whole** result so far. “Subtract 1, then double” is 2(n − 1), not 2n − 1.
- **Brackets:** If a step applies to a *sum or difference*, that part goes in brackets. Without them, only the last term gets the step.
- **“Squares” is a power:** **Squares** means “to the power 2” and **cubes** means “to the power 3”. A **square root** uses the root sign. Different words, different operations.
- **Rule from a table:** The value at *0* is the fixed charge. The jump from one column to the next is the amount for each extra unit. So cost = fixed + jump × units.
- **Check with a number:** Put a small number in for *n*, follow the words step by step, and see if the expression gives the same answer.

## Section B: Simplifying Surds

### Key facts

- **The splitting rule:** √(a × b) = √a × √b.
- **Use the largest square factor:** Look for the biggest of *4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100* that divides the number. Its root comes out as a whole number in front.
- **Check by squaring:** Square the number in front, then multiply by the number under the root. You should get the number you started with.
- **Adding surds:** Surds add like terms only when the part under the root matches: 3√2 + 4√2 = 7√2. Simplify each one first.
- **When it stays:** If no square number (except 1) divides it, the root is already in its simplest form.

## Section C: Surds in Brackets

### Key facts

- **Expand as usual:** Multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second. Remember √n × √n = n.
- **Difference of two squares:** (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b². The two middle terms are opposites, so they cancel.
- **Why it matters:** When the middle terms cancel, the surds vanish. The answer can be a whole number even though the brackets hold a surd.
- **No calculator:** Leave answers as surds or whole numbers. Never turn √n into a rounded decimal.

## Section D: Powers, Zero, Negative & Powers of 10

### Key facts

- **Use the table:** Powers of the same base: multiply means *add* the powers (7² × 7⁴ = 7⁶), divide means *subtract*. A table of powers lets you read the answer off.
- **Zero power:** Any number (except 0) to the power 0 is **1**.
- **Negative power:** x^(−n) = 1 ÷ x^(n). It is a *fraction*, not a negative number, and **x⁻¹ is not x**.
- **Multiplying by 10^(n):** Multiplying by 10³ makes a number 1000 times bigger. Multiplying by 10⁻³ is the same as *dividing* by 1000.
- **Dividing by 10^(−n):** Dividing by 10^(−n) is the same as *multiplying* by 10^(n). Two minuses make it bigger.

## Section F: Brackets, Signs & Equations

### Key facts

- **Two brackets:** Multiply every term by every term, then collect the like terms in the middle.
- **Signs:** A negative times a negative is positive. A minus in front of a bracket changes *every* sign inside it.
- **Subtracting equations:** Put the second equation in brackets first. The minus then applies to **every** term, including the right-hand side.
- **Then substitute back:** After finding one letter, put it into an original equation to find the other, then check it in the *other* equation.

## Section G: Mixed Numbers & Powers

### Key facts

- **Convert first:** Change mixed numbers to *improper fractions* before you square, multiply or divide. Never square the whole part and the fraction part separately.
- **Dividing:** To divide by a fraction, flip the second fraction and multiply.
- **Order of working:** Powers first, then multiplication and division, then adding and subtracting.

## Section H: Rational & Irrational Numbers

### Key facts

- **The pizza picture:** A **rational** number is a pizza you can cut fairly: 3 slices out of 4 is 3/4. An **irrational** number can never be cut fairly, however small the slices. There is always a crumb left over. π is that impossible pizza.
- **Rational:** Can be written as a fraction of two integers (the bottom is not 0). Whole numbers, terminating decimals like 0.25 and recurring decimals like 0.2525… are all rational.
- **Irrational:** Cannot be written as a fraction. The decimal never ends and never repeats. Examples: π, √2, √8.
- **Roots that fool you:** √49 = 7 and ∛27 = 3 are whole numbers, so they are **rational**. √8 and ∛10 are not perfect roots, so they are **irrational**.
- **The Venn diagram:** Integers sit *inside* the rational numbers (every integer is a fraction over 1). Irrational numbers sit *outside* the rational circle altogether.
