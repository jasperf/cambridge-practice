## Section A: Atoms & the Periodic Table

**Q1** (1 mark): 40 − 18 = 22 neutrons.

**Q2** (1 mark): 2 + 8 + 2 = 12 electrons, so there are 12 protons (it is magnesium).

**Q3** (1 mark): 2+: it loses its 2 outer electrons, leaving 12 protons and 10 electrons.

**Q4** (1 mark): **B**: A sodium ion, Na⁺. Eleven protons means sodium. It has one electron fewer than it has protons, so it is a 1+ ion. A neon atom has 10 protons and Mg²⁺ has 12.

## Section B: Bonding & Chemical Structures

**Q5** (1 mark): K₂S: two K⁺ ions balance one S²⁻ ion.

**Q6** (1 mark): Sodium oxide (Na₂O).

**Q7** (1 mark): 3 bonds × 2 electrons = 6 electrons.

**Q8** (1 mark): **D**: Layers of copper ions with free electrons moving between them. Copper has metallic bonding: layers of positive ions in a sea of free (delocalised) electrons. The free electrons carry the current, and the layers can slide without the bonds breaking.

**Q9** (2 marks): **A**: The weak forces between water molecules. Boiling only separates the molecules from each other, by overcoming the weak forces between them. The strong covalent bonds inside each molecule stay intact, which is why steam is still H₂O.

## Section C: Photosynthesis & Minerals

**Q10** (1 mark): The chloroplast. It contains chlorophyll, which absorbs the light.

**Q11** (2 marks): 48 ÷ 2 = 24 per minute; 30 ÷ 2 = 15 per minute; 24 − 15 = 9 more bubbles per minute.

**Q12** (1 mark): **C**: The temperature of the water. The water temperature must be kept the same. The lamp distance (and so the light brightness) is what is changed, and the bubble count is what is measured.

**Q13** (1 mark): Carbon dioxide.

**Q14** (2 marks): 30 − 12 = 18 g stored. 18 ÷ 30 × 100 = 60%.

## Section D: Transport, Transpiration & the Carbon Cycle

**Q15** (1 mark): Stomata (one is a stoma).

**Q16** (1 mark): **A**: It gives a large surface area for taking in water and minerals. A long, thin shape gives a large surface area touching the soil water, so water (by osmosis) and minerals are absorbed faster.

**Q17** (1 mark): 18 ÷ 6 = 3 g per hour.

**Q18** (1 mark): Arrow 3: it is the only numbered arrow that takes carbon out of the air (into plants).

**Q19** (1 mark): **D**: Combustion. Burning (combustion) fossil fuels releases the carbon stored in them as carbon dioxide.

## Section E: Human Biology: Excretion & Inheritance

**Q20** (1 mark): The ureters (one from each kidney).

**Q21** (1 mark): **B**: Breathing out carbon dioxide made in respiration. Carbon dioxide is a waste product of respiration in cells, so breathing it out is excretion. Faeces are undigested food that never entered the cells, so that is egestion, not excretion.

**Q22** (1 mark): 78 ÷ 2 = 39 pairs.

**Q23** (1 mark): 50%. Each pregnancy is independent: half the sperm carry X and half carry Y, whatever happened before.

**Q24** (2 marks): **C**: Carbon dioxide and urea. The fetus’s waste (carbon dioxide and urea) diffuses into the mother’s blood. Oxygen and glucose go the other way, blood cells do not cross, and alcohol and nicotine pass from mother to fetus.

## Section F: Density

**Q25** (1 mark): 7.9 × 20 = 158 g.

**Q26** (2 marks): Volume = 47 − 35 = 12 cm³. ρ = 30 ÷ 12 = 2.5 g/cm³.

**Q27** (1 mark): **C**: Resting on top of the syrup layer. The grape (1.1 g/cm³) is denser than oil and water, so it sinks through both, but it is less dense than syrup (1.4 g/cm³), so it stops on top of the syrup.

## Section G: Sound & Waves

**Q29** (1 mark): One complete wave covers 8 squares, so the wavelength is 8 cm.

**Q30** (1 mark): The crests are 2 cm above the middle line, so the amplitude is 2 cm. (Top to bottom is 4 cm, which is twice the amplitude.)

**Q31** (1 mark): 600 ÷ 2 = 300 Hz.

**Q32** (1 mark): **A**: Its frequency increases. A higher pitch means a higher frequency. The amplitude stays the same because the loudness is unchanged, the wavelength gets shorter (not longer), and the speed of sound in air does not change.

**Q33** (1 mark): 4 − 1.5 = 2.5 cm (still a crest, as the crest was bigger).

## Section H: Thermal Energy & Conservation

**Q34** (1 mark): **B**: Conduction and convection. Conduction and convection both need particles, and a vacuum has none. Radiation can cross a vacuum, which is why flasks also have shiny silvered walls to reflect it.

**Q35** (1 mark): 200 − 30 − 50 = 120 J.

**Q36** (2 marks): Useful = 30 + 50 = 80 J. 80 ÷ 200 × 100 = 40%.

**Q37** (1 mark): **D**: The iceberg, because it has an enormous number of particles. The coffee is hotter, but the iceberg has vastly more particles, so its total thermal energy is far greater. Anything above absolute zero has thermal energy, including ice.

## Section I: Electricity

**Q38** (2 marks): Total R = 2 + 4 = 6 Ω. I = 12 ÷ 6 = 2 A.

**Q39** (1 mark): V = 2 × 4 = 8 V. (The 2 Ω resistor takes the other 4 V; 8 + 4 = 12 V.)

**Q40** (2 marks): 0.6 + 0.9 = 1.5 A.

**Q41** (1 mark): **A**: It doubles. With R fixed, I = V ÷ R, so doubling V doubles I.

**Q42** (1 mark): The ohm (Ω).
