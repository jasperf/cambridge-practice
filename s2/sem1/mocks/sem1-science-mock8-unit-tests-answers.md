## Section A: Electricity: Series Circuits, Symbols & Resistance

**Q1** (1 mark): 0.3 A. A series circuit has only one path, so the same current flows through every part of it.

**Q2** (1 mark): 6 − 3.6 = 2.4 V. In series the voltages across the components add up to the cell voltage.

**Q3** (1 mark): **D**: L2 goes out and both ammeters read 0 A, because there is now a gap in the only loop. In a series circuit there is only one path. The broken lamp leaves a gap, so no current flows anywhere in the loop: both lamps go out and both ammeters read zero. That is why old fairy lights wired in series all went out together.

**Q4** (1 mark): A variable resistor: a resistor with an arrow through it.

**Q5** (1 mark): An ammeter: a circle with an A inside. It measures current and goes in series.

**Q6** (1 mark): An open switch. The gap means no current can flow until it is closed.

**Q7** (1 mark): 4.5 ÷ 0.3 = 15 Ω.

**Q8** (1 mark): 20 Ω. For example 6 ÷ 0.3 = 20 Ω (every cross gives the same answer, because the line is straight through the origin).

**Q9** (2 marks): **C**: The lamps get dimmer, because the total resistance goes up, so less current flows. Each extra lamp in series adds its resistance to the one loop, so the total resistance rises and the current falls (I = V ÷ R). The 6 V is also shared between three lamps now, so each gets less. Try the dimmer explorer: more resistance in the loop, dimmer lamp.

## Section B: Inheritance, Cell Division & Pregnancy

**Q10** (1 mark): 32: half of 64, one from each pair. The egg also has 32, so the new cell has 64 again.

**Q11** (1 mark): A zygote (a fertilised egg). It divides again and again to become an embryo.

**Q12** (1 mark): Mitosis. It makes exact copies with the full number of chromosomes, for growth and repair. Meiosis is only for making sex cells.

**Q13** (1 mark): **B**: One zygote splits into two early on, so both babies carry copies of the same chromosomes. Identical twins come from one fertilised egg that splits in two, so each embryo has the same chromosomes. Two eggs fertilised by two sperm make non-identical twins, who are no more alike than other brothers and sisters. Genes come from the egg and the sperm, not from the placenta.

**Q14** (1 mark): X. Women are XX, so every egg gets an X. It is the sperm (X or Y) that decides the sex of the baby.

**Q15** (1 mark): 1. The sperm carries one sex chromosome (an X or a Y) and 22 others.

**Q16** (1 mark): The placenta. The two bloods do not mix: substances pass across thin walls between them.

**Q17** (1 mark): **A**: Carbon monoxide from the smoke joins to haemoglobin in the mother’s blood, so less oxygen reaches the fetus. Carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin in red blood cells in place of oxygen, so the mother’s blood carries less oxygen to the placenta. With less oxygen the fetus grows more slowly. The fetus does not breathe the smoke: its lungs are full of fluid until birth.

## Section C: Sound Waves

**Q18** (1 mark): X. It goes from one peak to the next peak.

**Q19** (1 mark): Y: from the middle line up to a peak. Z, from trough to peak, is twice the amplitude.

**Q20** (1 mark): 6 cm: arrow X spans 6 squares.

**Q21** (2 marks): frequency = 1 ÷ 0.004 = 250 Hz. In one second there is room for 250 vibrations that each last 0.004 s.

**Q22** (1 mark): **C**: The ringing gets quieter and quieter, because sound needs particles to pass it on. Sound is passed on by particles bumping into each other. With fewer and fewer air particles in the jar, less and less sound reaches the glass, so the ringing fades even though you can still see the hammer hitting the bell.

**Q23** (1 mark): **A**: Very little, because the two waves cancel out. A peak plus a trough of the same size adds up to almost nothing, so the waves cancel and P is a quiet spot. Where peaks meet peaks the sound is louder. Interference changes loudness, not pitch: the frequency stays the same.

**Q24** (1 mark): 5. The trace starts at a peak, so each complete wave runs from one peak to the next: 2 squares (2 ms). Peaks at 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 ms give 5 complete waves. Counting the peaks (6) instead of the gaps between them is the usual slip.

**Q25** (1 mark): 5 ÷ 0.01 = 500 Hz. (Or: one wave takes 2 ms = 0.002 s, and 1 ÷ 0.002 = 500 Hz.)

**Q26** (1 mark): **D**: The waves are taller, and the same number fit on the screen. Plucking harder makes the string vibrate with a bigger amplitude, so the sound is louder and the trace is taller. The same string at the same tension vibrates at the same frequency, so the number of waves on the screen (the pitch) does not change.

**Q27** (1 mark): One quarter. In 20 s, C makes 1 complete wave and D makes 4, so C has a quarter of the frequency of D and a lower pitch. (C is four times *taller*, but that is the amplitude, not the frequency.)

## Section D: Density

**Q28** (1 mark): The water collected is 12 cm³, the volume of all four keys. One key = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 cm³.

**Q29** (1 mark): 25.5 ÷ 3 = 8.5 g/cm³. (All four keys give the same answer: 102 ÷ 12 = 8.5 g/cm³.)

**Q30** (2 marks): Mass of oil = 0.92 × 250 = 230 g. Balance reading = 230 + 80 = 310 g.

**Q31** (1 mark): **B**: Water expands when it freezes, so ice is less dense than liquid water. Water is unusual: it expands as it freezes, so the same mass takes up more space. Ice (about 0.92 g/cm³) is less dense than water (1.00 g/cm³), so it floats. Most substances are denser as solids, and being cold does not decide floating.

**Q32** (1 mark): **D**: Between the oil and the water. 0.95 is more than oil (0.92), so the bead sinks through the oil, but less than water (1.00), so it floats on the water. It rests where the oil meets the water.

**Q33** (2 marks): Volume = 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cm³. Density = 81 ÷ 27 = 3 g/cm³.

## Section E: Thermal Energy & Investigations

**Q34** (1 mark): **C**: By radiation, because infrared waves need no particles to travel. Space is almost a vacuum, so there are no particles to pass on vibrations (conduction) or to flow (convection). Infrared radiation is a wave that can travel through a vacuum.

**Q35** (1 mark): Conduction. The particles at the hot end vibrate more and pass the vibrations along the metal.

**Q36** (1 mark): **A**: The fastest-moving water particles escape, so the particles left behind have less energy on average and take thermal energy from your skin. Only the most energetic particles escape from the surface. That lowers the average energy, and so the temperature, of the liquid left behind, which then takes thermal energy from your skin. Sweat starts at body temperature, not colder.

**Q37** (1 mark): The dull black can. Dull black surfaces give out infrared radiation faster than shiny ones; shiny surfaces reflect it back in.

**Q38** (1 mark): 21.5 °C. The other three results are all close to 14 °C.

**Q39** (1 mark): (14.2 + 13.8 + 14.0) ÷ 3 = 42.0 ÷ 3 = 14.0 °C.

**Q40** (2 marks): **B**: The starting temperature and the volume of water in each can. Only the surface (black or shiny) should change. If one can held more water or started hotter, that alone could change how fast it cools. The colour is the variable she changes on purpose, and the temperature drop is what she measures.
