## Section A: Electricity: Current, Symbols & Resistance

### Key facts

- **Current in branches:** At a junction the current *splits* between the branches, then joins up again. The branch currents add up to the current in the main wire.
- **Meters:** An *ammeter* goes in series, in the wire whose current you want. A *voltmeter* goes across a component, in parallel.
- **Ohm’s law:** I = V ÷ R   current in amps, voltage in volts, resistance in ohms (Ω).
- **Series vs parallel:** Series: one loop, one path. Parallel: each component on its own branch with its own path back to the cell.

## Section B: Inheritance & Variation

### Key facts

- **Chromosomes:** Long threads of DNA inside the nucleus. A gene is a short section of a chromosome.
- **Halving and doubling:** Body cells hold chromosomes in pairs. Sex cells (gametes) hold *one of each pair*; fertilisation restores the full number.
- **Sex chromosomes:** Females XX, males XY. Every egg carries an X; a sperm carries an X or a Y.
- **Variation:** Some differences are inherited through genes; some come from the environment, i.e. how and where you live.

## Section C: Sound Waves

### Key facts

- **Loudness:** The *amplitude* is the height of a peak above the middle line. Bigger amplitude, louder sound.
- **Pitch:** *Frequency* is the number of waves per second, in hertz (Hz). More waves squeezed across the screen, higher pitch.
- **Longitudinal:** Sound travels as compressions (particles squashed together) and rarefactions (particles spread out).
- **Frequency sums:** frequency = number of waves ÷ time in s

## Section D: Density

### Key facts

- **The formula:** density = mass ÷ volume rearranged: mass = density × volume, volume = mass ÷ density
- **Volume of a stone:** Displacement: the rise in the water level in a measuring cylinder (1 mL = 1 cm³).
- **Mass of a liquid:** Weigh the container empty and full; the difference is the liquid.
- **Float or sink:** Less dense than the liquid: floats. Denser: sinks.

## Section E: Thermal Energy & Investigations

### Key facts

- **Temperature vs thermal energy:** Temperature (°C) is the *average* energy of the particles. Thermal energy (J) is the *total*, so it depends on the amount too.
- **Convection:** Only in liquids and gases. Warmer fluid expands, becomes less dense and rises; cooler fluid sinks.
- **Precise:** Repeat readings close together (a small range).
- **Accurate:** Readings close to the true value. A thermometer that is wrong by the same amount each time can be precise but not accurate.
