## Section A: Atoms & the Periodic Table

**Q1** (1 mark): 23 − 11 = 12 neutrons.

## Section B: Bonding & Chemical Structures

**Q4** (1 mark): CaF₂: one 2+ ion needs two 1− ions to make the charges total zero.

**Q5** (1 mark): **D**: Giant metallic Metals conduct as solids because of delocalised electrons. Ionic solids cannot conduct until melted, simple molecules never conduct, and diamond has no free electrons.

**Q6** (1 mark): A double bond shares 2 pairs of electrons.

**Q7** (2 marks): **A**: Every carbon atom is joined to four others by strong covalent bonds all through the structure Diamond is one giant network of strong covalent bonds, and a lot of energy is needed to break them. It has no separate molecules, no ions and no free electrons.

## Section C: Photosynthesis & Minerals

**Q8** (1 mark): Chlorophyll.

**Q9** (1 mark): **C**: Magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll Magnesium goes into chlorophyll. Without it the leaves cannot make enough chlorophyll, so they turn yellow and photosynthesise less.

**Q10** (2 marks): 9.2 − 8.0 = 1.2. 1.2 ÷ 8.0 × 100 = 15%.

## Section D: Transport, Transpiration & the Carbon Cycle

**Q11** (1 mark): The xylem.

**Q12** (1 mark): **A**: Hot, windy and dry Heat, wind and dry air all speed up evaporation from the leaf, so the plant loses water fastest.

**Q13** (1 mark): **D**: Photosynthesis Photosynthesis takes carbon dioxide out of the air. Respiration, combustion and decomposition all put it back.

## Section E: Human Biology: Excretion & Inheritance

**Q14** (1 mark): The liver.

**Q15** (1 mark): **B**: Urethra The urethra carries urine out of the bladder. The ureters carry it from the kidneys to the bladder.

**Q16** (1 mark): Half of 46 is 23.

**Q17** (1 mark): **C**: Whether the sperm that fertilises the egg carries an X or a Y Every egg carries X, but a sperm carries X or Y. An X sperm gives a girl (XX) and a Y sperm gives a boy (XY).

**Q18** (2 marks): **D**: Alcohol passes across the placenta into the fetus’s blood and can harm its development Harmful substances such as alcohol cross the placenta, even though the two bloods never mix, and can damage the fetus as it develops.

## Section G: Sound & Waves

**Q22** (1 mark): T = 1 ÷ 250 = 0.004 s.

**Q23** (1 mark): **C**: The vibrations are along the direction the wave travels In a longitudinal wave the particles vibrate backwards and forwards along the direction of travel, making compressions and rarefactions.

**Q24** (2 marks): **B**: The crest of one wave meets the trough of the other there, so they cancel (destructive interference) Waves that arrive out of step cancel each other out. This is destructive interference, so the sound is quiet.
