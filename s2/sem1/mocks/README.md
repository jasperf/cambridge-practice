# Secondary 2 · Semester 1 Assessment — Mock Papers

Full-length practice papers for the Semester 1 assessments, **Monday 23 – Friday 27 November 2026** (each exam finishes at 11:00). Each paper pulls together every topic taught in Term 1 and Term 2 so far, with all-new questions.

Every paper comes in three files with the same basename:

- **`.html`**: the interactive sheet. Every question is auto-marked (multiple choice, a typed number or a typed word). Diagrams draw themselves in as you scroll.
- **`.md`**: the printable paper. It has the same questions in the same order, plus **written questions** at the end that are not on the interactive sheet (explanations, P.E.E.E. paragraphs, constructions, extended writing). These are marked by the teacher.
- **`-answers.md`**: answers to every auto-marked question and a marking guide for the written questions. It is kept separate, so the question paper can be handed out on its own.

### Mathematics

The two papers follow the Cambridge Lower Secondary Checkpoint maths format (0862): **Paper 1 is non-calculator and Paper 2 allows a calculator. Each is 1 hour long, worth 50 marks, and mixes every unit.** So both papers test Unit 1 (rational and irrational numbers, indices, standard form), Unit 2 (expressions and formulae), Unit 3 (decimals, percentages, rounding and bounds), Unit 4 (simultaneous equations, inequalities), Unit 5 (angles, polygons, constructions, Pythagoras) and Unit 7.1–7.2 (circles, compound shapes). Questions that need a calculator, such as compound interest, Pythagoras with decimals and circle areas to 1 d.p., are in Paper 2. Paper 1 uses equivalent calculations, answers in terms of π and π ≈ 3. Each paper has a Checkpoint-style “tick every true statement” question, which scores only if every tick is right. Diagrams draw themselves in as you scroll: number lines, an area model, a lift loaded with boxes, a pair of lines crossing on a graph, parallel lines, polygons, a ladder, circles and compound shapes. They are not to scale. Paper 1’s written questions include a ruler-and-compass construction. **Paper 1B** is a second non-calculator paper for another round of practice. It has the same sections and marks as Paper 1, but every question is new (e.g. alternate angles, an isosceles triangle, a regular octagon, a rectangle with a semicircle). **Paper 1C** is a third set in the same format (e.g. corresponding angles, a regular pentagon, a quarter circle, a running-track shape).

For revision before the maths papers, the [maths formula sheets](../../reference/maths-formula-sheets/README.md) now include one-page sheets for expanding brackets, simultaneous equations, indices and standard form, inequalities, decimals and bounds, and formulae with algebraic fractions (Sheets 7–12).

Section E is simple and giant structures (diamond, graphite and silicon dioxide). **37 questions, 49 marks, 14 MCQ**, all auto-marked. Three explorers come **after** their section's questions, so they cannot give answers away: a **Kidney Filter** (blood particles sorted into blood or urine, with a normal, hot or well-watered day), a **Transpiration Lab** (temperature, wind, humidity and day or night) and **Run the Carbon Cycle** (move carbon between five stores and watch the CO₂ in the air). The printable adds six written questions (Q38–Q43, 21 marks): drawing the urinary system, water's journey from soil to air, drawing a carbon cycle, planning a wind-and-transpiration investigation, a Sankey diagram for a filament bulb, and diamond versus graphite.

The class slides do not cover most of these topics, so the facts follow the Cambridge Lower Secondary Stage 9 syllabus; check them against your class notes. Built from `scripts/review_specs/s2_sem1_science_gaps.py`.
