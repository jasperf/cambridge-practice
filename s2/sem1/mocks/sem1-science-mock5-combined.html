Section A · Q1–Q4 · Chemical structures & properties

Atoms & the Periodic Table

Full sheet →
Key facts

Inside an atom

Protons (+1) and neutrons (no charge) sit in the nucleus; electrons (−1) move in shells around it. A neutral atom has as many electrons as protons.

Two numbers

atomic number = protons
mass number = protons + neutrons

Filling shells

For the first 20 elements the shells hold up to 2, 8, 8, 2, filled from the inside out.

Reading the table

Group = electrons in the outer shell. Period = number of shells in use.

Ions

Atoms lose or gain electrons to get a full outer shell. Losing electrons gives a positive ion; gaining gives a negative ion. The number of protons never changes.

Section B · Q5–Q9 · Chemical bonding · giant structures

Bonding & Chemical Structures

Full sheet →
Key facts

Ionic bonding

Metal + non-metal. Electrons move from the metal to the non-metal; the oppositely charged ions attract. Groups 1/2/3 form 1+/2+/3+ ions; Groups 5/6/7 form 3−/2−/1−.

Naming and formulae

Metal first, non-metal ending changed to -ide. Total positive charge = total negative charge: Al³⁺ and Cl⁻ give AlCl₃.

Covalent bonding

Non-metals share pairs of electrons. Each shared pair is one single bond.

Metallic bonding

Positive metal ions with electrons that are free to move through the whole structure.

Simple vs giant

Simple molecules: strong bonds inside each molecule, but low melting and boiling points. Giant covalent structures (diamond, graphite, SiO₂): very high melting points.

The equation

Uses of glucose

Respiration, and turned into starch (storage), cellulose (cell walls), proteins, and fats and oils.

Minerals

Section D · Q15–Q19 · Plant biology · gap topic

Transport, Transpiration & the Carbon Cycle

Full sheet →
Key facts

Xylem and phloem

Xylem carries water and minerals up from the roots. Phloem carries dissolved sugar to wherever it is needed.

Root hairs

Water enters the root hair cells from the soil by osmosis; minerals are absorbed there too.

Transpiration

Water evaporates from the leaf and escapes as vapour through tiny pores opened and closed by guard cells. Faster when hot, windy, dry and bright.

Carbon cycle

Photosynthesis takes carbon dioxide out of the air. Respiration, decomposition and burning put it back. Eating passes carbon along food chains; fossil fuels lock it away for millions of years.

Section E · Q20–Q24 · Human biology · gap topic

Human Biology: Excretion & Inheritance

Full sheet →
Key facts

Excretion

Getting rid of waste made by the body’s own cells. The liver turns spare amino acids into urea, which the kidneys remove.

Urinary system

Kidneys filter the blood and make urine, which is stored in the bladder and leaves through the urethra.

Chromosomes

Genes are sections of DNA on chromosomes, which come in pairs. Gametes (egg and sperm) have half the usual number.

Boy or girl

XX is female and XY is male. Every egg carries X; a sperm carries X or Y, so each baby has a 50% chance of either sex.

The placenta

The mother’s and fetus’s bloods come very close in the placenta but never mix. Substances diffuse across, both ways.

Section F · Q25–Q28 · Chemical structures · properties

Density

Full sheet →
Key facts

The formula

ρ = m ÷ V, rearranged to m = ρ × V and V = m ÷ ρ.

Volumes

Regular block: length × width × height. Odd shape: measure how far the water rises in a measuring cylinder (1 mL = 1 cm³).

Units

1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³. Water is 1 g/cm³.

Float or sink

An object floats on a liquid that is denser than it, and sinks in a liquid that is less dense.

Section G · Q29–Q33 · Sound

Sound & Waves

Full sheet →
Key facts

Measuring a wave

Amplitude: from the middle line to the top of a crest. Wavelength: the length of one complete wave. Frequency: waves per second, in hertz (Hz).

Period

T = 1 ÷ f and f = 1 ÷ T.

Pitch and loudness

Larger amplitude: louder. Higher frequency: higher pitch.

Interference

Where waves meet, their displacements add. Crest + crest: bigger (constructive). Crest + trough: smaller or cancelled (destructive).

Section H · Q34–Q37 · Thermal energy

Thermal Energy & Conservation

Full sheet →
Key facts

Temperature vs thermal energy

Temperature measures how hot something is (the average energy of its particles). Thermal energy also depends on how many particles there are.

Three transfers

Conduction passes energy particle to particle. Convection is a flow of warm liquid or gas. Radiation (infrared) needs no particles at all.

Conservation of energy

Energy is never created or destroyed. energy in = useful energy out + wasted energy out.

Efficiency

efficiency = useful energy out ÷ total energy in × 100%.

Section I · Q38–Q42 · Electricity

Electricity

Full sheet →
Key facts

Ohm’s law

V = I × R, so I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I.

Series

One loop: the current is the same everywhere and resistances add up.

Parallel

Each branch gets the full battery voltage, and the branch currents add up to the current from the battery.

Meters

An ammeter is connected in series; a voltmeter in parallel, across the component.
