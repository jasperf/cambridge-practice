Section A · Q1–Q3 · Chemical structures & properties

Atoms & the Periodic Table

Full sheet →
Key facts

Inside an atom

Protons (+1) and neutrons (no charge) in the nucleus; electrons (−1) in shells around it. In a neutral atom, electrons = protons.

Two numbers

atomic number = protons
mass number = protons + neutrons

Filling shells

For the first 20 elements the shells hold 2, 8, 8, 2. Phosphorus (15) is 2,8,5: 2 + 8 + 5.

Reading the table

Group = electrons in the outer shell. Period = shells in use. Ions have full outer shells, so count their electrons before working out numbers.

Trends

Group 1 metals lose their outer electron and are more reactive going down. Group 7 non-metals gain one electron and are less reactive going down.

Section B · Q4–Q7 · Chemical bonding · giant structures

Bonding & Chemical Structures

Full sheet →
Key facts

Ionic bonding

Metal + non-metal. Electrons move from metal to non-metal; the opposite charges attract. Groups 1/2/3 form 1+/2+/3+ ions; Groups 5/6/7 form 3−/2−/1−.

Naming ionic compounds

Metal first, then the non-metal with its name changed: lithium bromide, calcium chloride.

Writing formulae

Total + = total −. Al³⁺ and O²⁻ give Al₂O₃; Li⁺ and Br⁻ give LiBr.

Covalent bonding

Non-metal + non-metal share pairs of electrons. A single bond = 1 pair, a double bond = 2 pairs. In CO₂ the carbon makes two double bonds.

Metallic bonding

Positive ions in a sea of delocalised electrons — which is what carries a current.

Simple vs giant

Simple molecules (H₂O, CH₄) have weak forces between molecules and low melting points. Giant covalent structures (diamond, graphite, SiO₂) have strong bonds throughout, so very high melting points. Graphite has one free electron per atom and weak forces between its sheets.

Photosynthesis & Minerals

Full sheet →
Key facts

The equation

carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, with light energy absorbed by chlorophyll in the chloroplasts.

Uses of glucose

Respiration, and turned into starch (storage), cellulose (cell walls), proteins and fats and oils.

Minerals

Magnesium goes into chlorophyll; nitrate makes protein. Fertiliser raises the yield.

Starch test

Boil the leaf in water, warm it in ethanol (to remove the green colour), rinse, add iodine: blue-black = starch is present.

Light investigation

Pondweed bubbles oxygen faster with more light: move the lamp closer and count the bubbles per minute, keeping everything else the same.

Section D · Q11–Q13 · Plant biology · gap topic

Transport, Transpiration & the Carbon Cycle

Full sheet →
Key facts

Xylem

Hollow tubes of dead cells carrying water and minerals up from the roots.

Phloem

Tubes of living cells carrying dissolved sugar from the leaves to wherever it is needed, up or down.

Transpiration

Water evaporates inside the leaf and leaves as vapour through the stomata. Root hairs take water in by osmosis; the xylem stream pulls more water up.

Stomata

Opened and closed by guard cells. Faster water loss when it is hotter, windier, drier and brighter. Many close in the dark.

Carbon cycle

Photosynthesis takes carbon dioxide out of the air. Respiration, decomposition (by bacteria and fungi) and combustion put it back. Feeding passes carbon along food chains; fossil fuels store it.

Section E · Q14–Q18 · Human biology · gap topic

Human Biology: Excretion & Inheritance

Full sheet →
Key facts

Excretion

Removing waste made by the body’s own cells. The lungs excrete CO₂; the liver turns spare amino acids into urea; the kidneys put urea into urine.

Urinary system

Kidneys filter blood (renal artery in, renal vein out) → each kidney’s urine goes down a ureter to the bladder, out through the urethra.

Nephrons

Filter everything except cells and protein, then take back all the glucose, most water and the salts the body needs.

DNA, genes, chromosomes

DNA is the twisted-ladder molecule; a gene is a section that codes for one feature; genes sit on chromosomes in the nucleus.

Pregnancy

The fetus grows in the uterus, cushioned by amniotic fluid, and is joined to the placenta by the umbilical cord. Oxygen and nutrients pass from the mother’s blood to the fetus’s blood, and carbon dioxide and urea pass back. The bloods never mix, but harmful substances (nicotine, carbon monoxide, alcohol, some viruses) can cross too.

Inheritance

Body cells have 46 chromosomes (23 pairs). Meiosis makes gametes with 23 each. Egg always carries X; sperm carries X or Y, so the sperm decides the sex: XX girl, XY boy — a 50/50 chance.

Section F · Q19–Q21 · Chemical structures · properties

Density

Full sheet →
Key facts

The formula

ρ = m ÷ V, rearranged to m = ρ × V and V = m ÷ ρ.

Volumes

Regular block: l × w × h. Odd object: drop it in a measuring cylinder; the rise in mL is its volume in cm³ (1 mL = 1 cm³).

Units

1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³. Water is 1 g/cm³.

Float or sink

Less dense than the liquid: floats. Denser: sinks. A solid can float on a denser liquid (iron floats on mercury).

Heating

Particles spread out: mass unchanged, volume bigger, so density falls — which is why warm air rises.

Section G · Q22–Q24 · Sound

Sound & Waves

Full sheet →
Key facts

Two kinds of wave

Transverse: vibrations at right angles to travel (light, water ripples). Longitudinal: vibrations along the direction of travel (sound: compressions and rarefactions).

Measurements

Amplitude: middle line to a crest. Wavelength: crest to crest. Frequency: waves per second, in Hz.

Period

T = 1 ÷ f. A wave with period 0.005 s has f = 1 ÷ 0.005 = 200 Hz.

Loud and quiet

Bigger amplitude = louder. Higher frequency = higher pitch.

Superposition

Crest meets crest: add. Crest meets trough: subtract or cancel.

Interference

Two waves meeting add up. In step (crest with crest): constructive, a bigger wave and a louder sound. Out of step (crest with trough): destructive, they cancel and the sound gets quieter.

Section H · Q25–Q27 · Thermal energy

Thermal Energy & Conservation

Full sheet →
Key facts

Heat flows hot → cold

Temperature is the average energy of the particles; thermal energy is the total and depends on mass too.

Three transfers

Conduction: particle to particle, best in metals. Convection: warm liquid or gas rises, cool sinks. Radiation: infrared needs no particles, so it crosses empty space.

Evaporation

The fastest particles escape from a surface at any temperature, cooling what is left behind.

Conservation of energy

Energy is never created or destroyed — only transferred. energy in = useful out + wasted out, and the waste ends up heating the surroundings.

Sankey diagrams

The arrow widths show the amounts. Everything out must add up to what went in.

Section I · Q28–Q30 · Electricity

Electricity

Full sheet →
Key facts

Ohm’s law

V = I × R, so I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I. Volts, amps, ohms.

Series

One loop, same current everywhere, voltages add, R = R₁ + R₂. Break anywhere and everything stops.

Parallel

Each branch gets the full voltage; branch currents add up. A break in one branch leaves the others working.

Circuit symbols

Cell: a long and a short line. Switch: a gap with a lever. Lamp: a circle with a cross. Resistor: a rectangle. Ammeter: a circle with A. Voltmeter: a circle with V.

Meters

Ammeter in series; voltmeter in parallel across the component.
