## Section A: Atoms & the Periodic Table

**Q1** (1 mark): 23 − 11 = 12 neutrons.

**Q2** (1 mark): 2,8,7: 2 + 8 = 10 in the first two shells, leaving 7 in the third.

**Q3** (1 mark): The atom has 12 electrons; the ion loses 2, so it has 10: a full 2,8 outer shell.

**Q4** (2 marks): **C**: Fluorine: Group 7, Period 2 Fluorine is at the top of Group 7. Its outer shell is closest to the nucleus, so it pulls in an extra electron most strongly. Reactivity falls as you go down: Cl, Br, I.

## Section B: Bonding & Chemical Structures

**Q5** (1 mark): Magnesium oxide (MgO).

**Q6** (1 mark): **A**: Methane (CH₄) Methane is carbon and hydrogen, both non-metals, so the atoms share electrons. The other three are a metal plus a non-metal, so they are ionic.

**Q7** (1 mark): A double bond is 2 shared pairs of electrons.

**Q8** (1 mark): CaCl₂: one 2+ ion needs two 1− ions to balance.

**Q9** (2 marks): **D**: A giant ionic lattice The high melting point shows strong bonds throughout, and the ions are only free to carry a current once the lattice is molten. A metal would conduct as a solid, and a giant covalent structure would not conduct at all.

**Q10** (2 marks): **B**: Each carbon atom makes four strong covalent bonds, joining all the atoms into one continuous network To scratch diamond you have to break a huge number of strong covalent bonds, because every atom is bonded to four others in a continuous network.

## Section C: Photosynthesis & Minerals

**Q11** (1 mark): Chlorophyll.

**Q12** (1 mark): **D**: Magnesium is needed to make chlorophyll, so less light can be absorbed Magnesium is part of the chlorophyll molecule. Without it the leaf cannot make enough green pigment, so it turns pale and photosynthesises less.

**Q13** (1 mark): **A**: To remove the green colour, so the blue-black colour can be seen Ethanol dissolves out the chlorophyll. Without it, a blue-black result on a green leaf would be hard to see.

## Section E: Human Biology: Excretion & Inheritance

**Q22** (1 mark): The liver.

**Q23** (2 marks): **A**: Glucose Glucose is small enough to be filtered, but it is all reabsorbed, so a healthy person has none in their urine. Urea stays in the urine. Protein and blood cells are too big to be filtered in the first place.

**Q24** (1 mark): Half of 40: an egg cell carries 20 chromosomes.

**Q25** (1 mark): Meiosis.

**Q26** (1 mark): **C**: An X chromosome A girl is XX. Her mother gave her an X in the egg, so the second X must have come from her father’s sperm.

**Q27** (2 marks): 750 ÷ 2 = 375 boys (and about 375 girls).

**Q28** (1 mark): Amniotic fluid.

**Q29** (2 marks): **D**: Some viruses are small enough to cross the placenta into the fetus’s blood The placenta is a thin exchange surface. The two bloods stay separate, but small things (oxygen, nutrients, and also some viruses and drugs) can cross it.

**Q30** (2 marks): **B**: It passes from the fetus’s blood across the placenta into the mother’s blood, and she excretes it Waste such as carbon dioxide and urea diffuses across the placenta from the fetus’s blood to the mother’s, and her lungs and kidneys remove it.

## Section F: Density

**Q31** (2 marks): V = 10 × 5 × 2 = 100 cm³, so ρ = 190 ÷ 100 = 1.9 g/cm³.

**Q32** (1 mark): 52 − 40 = 12 mL, so the volume is 12 cm³.

**Q33** (1 mark): 8000 ÷ 1000 = 8 g/cm³.

**Q34** (1 mark): **B**: It sinks, because its density is 1.25 g/cm³ ρ = 50 ÷ 40 = 1.25 g/cm³. That is more than water’s 1 g/cm³, so it sinks.

**Q35** (2 marks): **A**: Cold air is denser than warm air, so it sinks and cools the food below The air next to the freezer cools, contracts and becomes denser than the air around it, so it sinks and cools everything below. Warmer air rises to take its place.

## Section G: Sound & Waves

**Q36** (1 mark): T = 1 ÷ 250 = 0.004 s.

**Q37** (1 mark): **D**: The frequency of the vibration must increase Pitch depends on frequency: more vibrations per second gives a higher note. Amplitude changes the loudness, not the pitch.

**Q38** (1 mark): **C**: Sound from a guitar string Sound is made of compressions and rarefactions along the direction of travel. The others vibrate at right angles to their direction of travel, so they are transverse.

**Q39** (1 mark): 5 − 2 = 3 cm, still a crest.

**Q40** (1 mark): **A**: Almost no sound, because the waves cancel A crest meeting an equal trough cancels out: destructive interference, so the sound is quiet or silent there.

**Q41** (2 marks): **D**: At some spots the two waves arrive in step and add up; at others they arrive out of step and cancel The two waves have travelled different distances. Where crests meet crests the sound is louder (constructive); where crests meet troughs it is quieter (destructive).

## Section H: Thermal Energy & Conservation

**Q42** (2 marks): **B**: It traps air, and air is a poor conductor, so less heat is conducted away from your body The tiny pockets of trapped air are poor conductors, so heat escapes from your body slowly.

**Q43** (1 mark): **C**: Convection Water at the bottom warms, expands and rises, while cooler, denser water sinks to take its place. That circulation is convection.

**Q44** (1 mark): Conduction.

**Q45** (2 marks): 600 − 360 − 150 = 90 J.

**Q46** (2 marks): 4 ÷ 50 × 100 = 8%. The other 46 J heats the surroundings.

## Section I: Electricity

**Q47** (2 marks): R = 4 + 8 = 12 Ω, so I = 6 ÷ 12 = 0.5 A.

**Q48** (1 mark): R = 12 ÷ 3 = 4 Ω.

**Q49** (1 mark): 0.3 + 0.5 = 0.8 A.

**Q50** (1 mark): **A**: It stays lit at the same brightness Each branch is a separate loop with the full battery voltage across it. Breaking one branch does not affect the other.

**Q51** (1 mark): **B**: In parallel, across the lamp A voltmeter connects across the component, in parallel. An ammeter is the meter that goes in series.

**Q52** (2 marks): **C**: X is a resistor and Y is an ammeter A rectangle is the symbol for a resistor, and a circle with A is an ammeter. A lamp is a circle with a cross, a voltmeter has a V, and a cell is a long and a short line.
