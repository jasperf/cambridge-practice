Electricity: Meters, Symbols & Resistance

Full sheet →
Key facts

Two meters

An ammeter measures current and goes in the main loop, in series. A voltmeter measures the voltage across a component, so it is connected across it, in parallel.

Symbols to know

Cell, battery, switch, lamp, fixed resistor, variable resistor, ammeter (A in a circle) and voltmeter (V in a circle).

Resistance

Measured in ohms, Ω. For the same voltage, more resistance means less current.

Resistors side by side

Each extra parallel branch gives the current another path, so the total resistance of the circuit goes down.

Explorer: add resistors in parallel

Each resistor is 240 Ω. Add branches and watch the total resistance and the current from a 12 V battery.

Inheritance & Variation

Full sheet →
Key facts

Where the information is

The nucleus of a cell holds chromosomes. Chromosomes are made of DNA, and short sections of that DNA each help to decide one characteristic.

Pairs

Human body cells have 23 pairs of chromosomes. Sex cells carry just one chromosome from each pair, so fertilisation brings the number back to full.

Sex chromosomes

One of the 23 pairs: females are XX, males are XY.

Sound Waves

Full sheet →
Key facts

Loudness

The amplitude is the height of the wave from the middle line. Bigger amplitude, louder sound.

Pitch

The frequency is how many waves pass each second, in hertz (Hz). Higher frequency, higher pitch. On a trace, waves squeezed closer together mean a higher frequency.

How sound travels

A vibrating object pushes and pulls on the air particles next to it. The vibration is passed on as a longitudinal wave of compressions and rarefactions.

Counting waves

One complete wave is one peak and one trough. Count from a point to the next point at the same stage, e.g. peak to peak.

Frequency sums

frequency = number of waves ÷ time in s

The formula

Section E · Q34–Q40 · Unit test 2 · heat vs temperature · convection · precise vs accurate

Thermal Energy & Investigations

Full sheet →
Key facts

Temperature

Measured in °C. It tells you how fast the particles are moving on average.

Thermal energy

Measured in joules (J). It is the total energy of all the particles, so it depends on how many particles there are as well as how hot they are.

Convection

Happens in liquids and gases, where the particles can move from place to place. It sets up a loop of moving fluid.

Precise

Repeat readings that are close together.

Accurate

Readings that are close to the true value.
