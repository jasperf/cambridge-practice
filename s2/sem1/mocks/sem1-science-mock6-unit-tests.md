## Section A: Electricity: Meters, Symbols & Resistance

### Key facts

- **Two meters:** An *ammeter* measures current and goes in the main loop, in series. A *voltmeter* measures the voltage across a component, so it is connected across it, in parallel.
- **Symbols to know:** Cell, battery, switch, lamp, fixed resistor, variable resistor, ammeter (A in a circle) and voltmeter (V in a circle).
- **Resistance:** Measured in ohms, Ω. For the same voltage, more resistance means less current.
- **Resistors side by side:** Each extra parallel branch gives the current another path, so the total resistance of the circuit goes down.

## Section B: Inheritance & Variation

### Key facts

- **Where the information is:** The nucleus of a cell holds chromosomes. Chromosomes are made of DNA, and short sections of that DNA each help to decide one characteristic.
- **Pairs:** Human body cells have 23 pairs of chromosomes. Sex cells carry just one chromosome from each pair, so fertilisation brings the number back to full.
- **Sex chromosomes:** One of the 23 pairs: females are XX, males are XY.

## Section C: Sound Waves

### Key facts

- **Loudness:** The *amplitude* is the height of the wave from the middle line. Bigger amplitude, louder sound.
- **Pitch:** The *frequency* is how many waves pass each second, in hertz (Hz). Higher frequency, higher pitch. On a trace, waves squeezed closer together mean a higher frequency.
- **How sound travels:** A vibrating object pushes and pulls on the air particles next to it. The vibration is passed on as a longitudinal wave of compressions and rarefactions.
- **Counting waves:** One complete wave is one peak *and* one trough. Count from a point to the next point at the same stage, e.g. peak to peak.
- **Frequency sums:** frequency = number of waves ÷ time in s

## Section D: Density

### Key facts

- **The formula:** density = mass ÷ volume mass = density × volume
- **Units:** Mass in g and volume in cm³ give density in g/cm³.
- **Odd shapes:** Lower the object into water in a measuring cylinder. Its volume is how far the water level rises.
- **Many identical objects:** Measure them all together, take away the container, then divide by how many there are.

## Section E: Thermal Energy & Investigations

### Key facts

- **Temperature:** Measured in °C. It tells you how fast the particles are moving *on average*.
- **Thermal energy:** Measured in joules (J). It is the *total* energy of all the particles, so it depends on how many particles there are as well as how hot they are.
- **Convection:** Happens in liquids and gases, where the particles can move from place to place. It sets up a loop of moving fluid.
- **Precise:** Repeat readings that are close together.
- **Accurate:** Readings that are close to the true value.
