## Section A: The Excretory System

## Section B: Absorption, Transport & Transpiration

**Q9** (1 mark): Xylem: the coloured water was pulled up the xylem tubes.

**Q10** (1 mark): Phloem, which carries dissolved sugar from the leaves.

**Q11** (2 marks): **D**: With less surface area, the roots take in water more slowly than the leaves lose it. Root hairs give a huge surface for absorbing water. With fewer of them, water comes in more slowly than transpiration takes it out, so the cells lose water and the plant droops until new hairs grow.

**Q12** (1 mark): **A**: A hot, dry, windy afternoon Heat, dry air, wind and daylight (stomata open) all speed up transpiration. Every other choice has damp air, darkness, cold or no wind.

**Q13** (1 mark): 18 ÷ 6 = 3 mm per minute.

**Q14** (2 marks): 250.0 − 241.6 = 8.4 g in 4 hours, so 8.4 ÷ 4 = 2.1 g per hour.

**Q15** (2 marks): **C**: Most of the stomata are on the lower surface of the leaf. Blocking the bottom surface (R) stopped most of the water loss, but blocking the top (Q) made almost no difference. So most stomata are on the underside, which is shaded and cooler.

**Q16** (1 mark): Osmosis.

## Section C: The Carbon Cycle

**Q17** (1 mark): Photosynthesis. Respiration, decomposition and combustion all add carbon dioxide to the air.

**Q18** (1 mark): Decomposers: bacteria and fungi. They respire, releasing the carbon as carbon dioxide.

**Q19** (1 mark): **D**: Feeding The cow ate (fed on) the grass, digested it and used the carbon compounds to build its own body.

**Q20** (2 marks): **B**: It rises, because burning releases carbon dioxide and fewer trees are left to photosynthesise. Combustion releases the trees’ carbon as carbon dioxide, and with the trees gone, less carbon dioxide is taken back out by photosynthesis. Animals give out carbon dioxide, they do not take it in.

**Q21** (1 mark): **A**: They were buried where there was little oxygen, so decomposers could not break them down. Buried in mud and water with almost no oxygen, the plants did not decompose. Heat and pressure turned them into coal over millions of years.

**Q22** (2 marks): 420 − 280 = 140. 140 ÷ 280 × 100 = 50%.

**Q23** (1 mark): Purple: photosynthesis takes in carbon dioxide faster than respiration gives it out, so the level falls.

**Q24** (1 mark): Yellow: there is no photosynthesis in the dark, but the pondweed keeps respiring, so carbon dioxide builds up.

## Section D: Conservation of Energy

**Q25** (1 mark): 2400 − 2040 = 360 J wasted each second.

**Q26** (2 marks): 500 − 350 − 120 = 30 J of sound.

**Q27** (1 mark): **D**: The energy was transferred, mostly ending up as a little heat in the surroundings. Energy cannot be destroyed. The chemical store in the battery was transferred by electricity into light, sound and, in the end, the thermal store of the surroundings.

**Q28** (2 marks): **C**: It was transferred to the surroundings as heat and sound at each bounce. At each bounce some energy is transferred as sound and as heat (in the ball, the floor and the air). The total amount of energy is the same; it is just spread out.

**Q29** (1 mark): The chemical store of the batteries.

**Q30** (1 mark): **A**: The trapped gas is a poor conductor, so less heat is lost through the window by conduction. Gases are poor conductors. The gap is too narrow for much convection, so heat escapes through the window much more slowly.

## Section E: Simple & Giant Structures

**Q31** (1 mark): Giant covalent (it is silicon dioxide). Ionic compounds conduct when melted, and metals conduct as solids.

**Q32** (1 mark): Simple molecular (it is methane, CH₄). Only weak forces between the molecules have to be overcome.

**Q33** (2 marks): **B**: In graphite each atom uses only three outer electrons in bonds, so one per atom can move. Carbon has four outer electrons. In diamond all four are in bonds. In graphite only three are, so the fourth can move along the sheets and carry a current. Neither contains ions.

**Q34** (1 mark): **D**: Sheets of carbon atoms slide off, because the forces between the sheets are weak. The bonds inside each sheet are strong, but the sheets are only weakly held together. Rubbing slides sheets off onto the paper.

**Q35** (2 marks): Each Si makes 4 bonds to O. There are 2 O for every Si, so the 4 bonds are shared between 2 oxygens: 4 ÷ 2 = 2 bonds each, one to each of 2 silicon atoms.

**Q36** (1 mark): Diamond: its rigid network of strong bonds makes it the hardest natural substance.

**Q37** (2 marks): **A**: CO₂ is made of small separate molecules with weak forces between them; SiO₂ is one giant network of covalent bonds. Boiling CO₂ only overcomes the weak forces between molecules. Melting SiO₂ means breaking strong covalent bonds all through the giant structure. Both contain only covalent bonds.
