## Section A: Compound Shapes (π = 22/7)

### Key facts

- **Take π = 22/7:** Radii of 7, 14 and 21 make the 7 cancel, so the answers come out neat.
- **Circle parts:** Circumference = 2πr. A semicircle edge is πr and a quarter-circle edge is ½πr. Area = πr², so a semicircle is ½πr² and a quarter circle is ¼πr².
- **Perimeter:** The distance round the *outside* only. A straight edge where two parts join is inside the shape, so leave it out.
- **Radius, not diameter:** If the diameter is given, halve it first.
- **Cut-out pieces:** Area of a trapezium = ½ × (sum of the parallel sides) × height. For a shape with a piece cut out, find the whole area and subtract the piece.

## Section B: Angles

### Key facts

- **Sums to remember:** Triangle 180°, quadrilateral 360°, angles on a straight line 180°, angles round a point 360°.
- **Isosceles triangle:** The two base angles are equal.
- **Exterior angle of a triangle:** Equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.
- **Regular polygon:** Sum of interior angles = (n − 2) × 180°. Divide by n for one angle.
- **Parallel lines:** Co-interior angles add to 180°. Alternate and corresponding angles are equal.
- **Shapes fitting together:** Use one angle rule at each corner of the big shape to find what is left for the other piece.

## Section C: Simplifying Surds

### Key facts

- **Splitting rule:** √(a × b) = √a × √b. Use the largest square factor (4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100).
- **Check by squaring:** a√b is right if a² × b gives the number you started with.
- **Adding surds:** Simplify each one first. Then add only if the part under the root matches.
- **Squaring a surd:** (a√b)² = a² × b. And (√b + c)(√b − c) = b − c².

## Section D: Indices & Powers of 10

### Key facts

- **Zero and negative powers:** x⁰ = 1 and x^(−n) = 1 ÷ x^(n). A negative power is a *fraction*, not a negative number.
- **Dividing by 10^(−n):** Is the same as multiplying by 10^(n).
- **Laws:** Multiply: add the powers. Divide: subtract the powers. Power of a power: multiply the powers. The power outside a bracket applies to the number too.

## Section E: Expressions, Brackets & Equations

### Key facts

- **Words to expressions:** Do the steps in order and use brackets when a step applies to a sum or difference.
- **Rule from a table:** Cost = value at 0 + jump × number of units.
- **Two brackets:** Multiply every term by every term, then collect the middle terms.
- **Subtracting equations:** Put the second equation in brackets so the minus applies to every term.
