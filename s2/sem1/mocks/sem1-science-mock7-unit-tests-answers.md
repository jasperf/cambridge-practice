## Section A: Electricity: Current, Symbols & Resistance

**Q1** (1 mark): 0.9 − 0.4 = 0.5 A. The two branch currents add up to the current in the main wire.

**Q2** (1 mark): Parallel. Each lamp is on its own branch, so the current splits between them.

**Q3** (1 mark): **B**: L2 stays lit, because it is still in a complete loop with the cell. L2 is on its own branch, so it still has a complete loop through the cell. That is why house lights are wired in parallel. The reading on A1 goes *down* (to 0.5 A), not up, because only one branch now carries current.

**Q4** (1 mark): A (fixed) resistor: a plain rectangle.

**Q5** (1 mark): Voltmeter: a circle with a V inside. It measures voltage, in volts.

**Q6** (1 mark): A battery: two or more cells joined together.

**Q7** (1 mark): 6 ÷ 30 = 0.2 A.

**Q8** (1 mark): About 24 Ω (accept 22–26). It lies between 30 Ω (4 resistors) and 20 Ω (6 resistors); 120 ÷ 5 = 24 Ω.

**Q9** (2 marks): **D**: It goes up, because the new branch gives the current another path, so the total resistance falls. Every new parallel branch is another path for the current, so the total resistance of the circuit falls and more current flows from the cell. A1, in the main wire, reads more. Adding lamps in *series* would add resistance, but these are side by side.

## Section B: Inheritance & Variation

**Q10** (1 mark): XY: a Y from the sperm and an X from the egg.

**Q11** (1 mark): A boy, because the baby is XY.

**Q12** (1 mark): 50%. Every baby is a fresh chance: half the sperm carry X and half carry Y, whatever happened before.

**Q13** (1 mark): **A**: Each sister came from a different egg and sperm, so each got a different mix of her parents’ chromosomes. Every egg and every sperm carries a different half of the parent’s chromosomes, so brothers and sisters each get a different mix (only identical twins share the same genes). Both parents pass on genes, and every child has 46 chromosomes.

**Q14** (1 mark): DNA.

**Q15** (1 mark): The nucleus.

## Section C: Sound Waves

**Q18** (1 mark): K. It has the biggest amplitude (3 squares from the middle line).

**Q19** (1 mark): L. It has 4 complete waves across the screen, more than any other trace, so it has the highest frequency.

**Q20** (1 mark): M. Its amplitude is only 1 square.

**Q21** (2 marks): J and L. Both have an amplitude of 2 squares, so they are equally loud. L has a higher pitch, but pitch does not change loudness.

**Q22** (1 mark): **D**: It vibrates backwards and forwards, along the direction the wave travels. In a longitudinal wave the particles vibrate back and forth along the direction of travel, passing the energy on. They do not travel with the wave. Up-and-down vibration at right angles is a transverse wave, like a wave on a rope.

**Q23** (1 mark): A compression. (Q, where they are spread out, is a rarefaction.)

**Q24** (1 mark): 3. Each complete wave is one peak and one trough, and the wave is back to its starting point (middle line, going up) at about 0.67 s, 1.33 s and 2.0 s.

**Q25** (1 mark): 3 ÷ 2.0 = 1.5 Hz.

**Q26** (1 mark): 1.5 cm. The peak is 3 squares above the middle line, and 3 × 0.5 = 1.5 cm. (The arrow from trough to peak is 6 squares = 3 cm, which is twice the amplitude.)

**Q27** (1 mark): **B**: 256 complete waves pass a point every second. Frequency is the number of complete waves each second, so 256 Hz means 256 waves pass every second. Frequency tells you about pitch, not loudness, and it is not a length or a time for one wave.

## Section E: Thermal Energy & Investigations

**Q34** (1 mark): **A**: The bath can have more thermal energy, because it has far more particles, even though they move more slowly on average. The kettle is hotter, so its particles move faster on average. But the bath holds so many more particles that its *total* energy is far bigger. Being hotter does not mean having more thermal energy.

**Q35** (1 mark): **D**: The cool air is denser, so it sinks, and warmer air rises to take its place, setting up a convection current through the room. Cool air from the unit is denser than the warm room air, so it sinks. Warmer air rises to replace it and is cooled in turn, so a convection current cools the whole room. A heater works best low down for the same reason. Cold air does not rise, and air is a poor conductor.

**Q36** (1 mark): A solid. Its particles can only vibrate in place, so they cannot flow and carry energy round. Solids pass on heat by conduction.

**Q37** (1 mark): **B**: Bea. Bea’s three readings are within 0.5 °C of each other (precise), but they are about 8 °C below the true value (not accurate). Amir’s are precise *and* accurate; Cai’s are neither; Dina’s average to the true value but are spread out, so they are not precise.

**Q38** (1 mark): (96 + 104 + 100) ÷ 3 = 300 ÷ 3 = 100 °C.

**Q39** (1 mark): 107 − 88 = 19 °C. A big range like this shows the readings are not precise.

**Q40** (2 marks): **C**: Check the thermometer in melting ice, which should read 0 °C, and correct for the difference, or use a different thermometer. The error is the same every time, so repeating and taking the mean only gives the same wrong answer more reliably. Checking (calibrating) the thermometer against a known temperature, or swapping it, removes the error and moves the readings closer to the true value.
