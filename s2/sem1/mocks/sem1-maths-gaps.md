## Section A: Words to Expressions in *n*

### Key facts

- **Do the steps in order:** Each *then* works on the **whole** result so far. “Subtract 1, then double” is 2(n − 1), not 2n − 1.
- **Brackets:** If a step applies to a *sum or difference*, that part goes in brackets. Without them, only the last term gets the step.
- **“Squares” is a power:** **Squares** means “to the power 2” and **cubes** means “to the power 3”. A **square root** or **cube root** uses the root sign. They are different words and different operations.
- **Rule from a table:** The value at *0* is the fixed charge. The jump from one column to the next is the amount for each extra unit. So cost = fixed + jump × units.
- **Check with a number:** To test an expression, put in a small number for *n*, follow the words step by step, and see if both routes give the same answer.

## Section B: Simplifying Surds

### Key facts

- **The splitting rule:** √(a × b) = √a × √b. It works because squaring both sides gives a × b.
- **Use the largest square factor:** Look for the biggest of *4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100* that divides the number. The root of that square comes out as a whole number in front.
- **Check by squaring:** Square the number in front, then multiply by the number under the root. You should get the number you started with. Example: 2√5 → 2² × 5 = 20.
- **Adding surds:** Surds add like terms only when the part under the root matches: 3√2 + 4√2 = 7√2. Simplify each one first to see if they match.
- **When it stays:** If no square number (except 1) divides it, the root is already in its simplest form.

## Section C: Surds in Brackets

### Key facts

- **Expand as usual:** Multiply every term in the first bracket by every term in the second. Remember √n × √n = n.
- **Difference of two squares:** (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b². The two middle terms are opposites, so they cancel.
- **Why it matters:** When the middle terms cancel, the surds disappear. The answer can be a whole number even though the brackets contain a surd.
- **No calculator:** Leave answers as surds or whole numbers. Never turn √n into a rounded decimal.

## Section D: Zero, Negative & Powers of 10

### Key facts

- **Zero power:** Any number (except 0) to the power 0 is **1**.
- **Negative power:** x^(−n) = 1 ÷ x^(n). It is a *fraction*, not a negative number, and **x⁻¹ is not x**.
- **Multiplying by 10^(n):** Multiplying by 10³ makes the number bigger (digits move three places up). Multiplying by 10⁻³ is the same as *dividing* by 10³.
- **Dividing by 10^(−n):** Dividing by 10^(−n) is the same as *multiplying* by 10^(n). Two minuses make it bigger, not smaller.
- **Comparing:** The more negative the power, the *smaller* the number: 10⁻¹ = 0.1, 10⁻² = 0.01, and so on.

## Section E: Indices & Fractions

### Key facts

- **Three laws:** Multiply: *add* the powers. Divide: *subtract* the powers. Power of a power: *multiply* the powers.
- **Numbers and letters separately:** Work out the numbers first, then deal with each letter by itself.
- **Cube or square the whole bracket:** In (3m²)⁴ the power outside applies to the *3* as well as to the m².
- **A negative power in a fraction:** An answer such as y⁻⁵ is written as 1/y⁵ when the question wants a fraction.
- **“Show that” layout:** Top first, bottom second, then combine. Write each step on its own line.

## Section F: Brackets, Signs & Equations

### Key facts

- **Two brackets:** Multiply every term by every term, then collect the like terms in the middle. A grid or arrows help you not miss one.
- **Signs:** A negative times a negative is positive. A minus in front of a bracket changes *every* sign inside it.
- **Equivalent expressions:** Expand each one fully. Two expressions are equivalent only if they come out exactly the same.
- **Subtracting equations:** Put the second equation in brackets first. The minus then applies to **every** term, including the right-hand side.
- **Then substitute back:** After finding one letter, put it into either original equation to find the other, then check it in the *other* equation.

## Section G: Mixed Numbers & Powers

### Key facts

- **Convert first:** Change mixed numbers to *improper fractions* before you square, multiply or divide. Never square the whole part and the fraction part separately.
- **Dividing:** To divide by a fraction, flip the second fraction and multiply.
- **Order of working:** Powers first, then multiplication and division, then adding and subtracting.
