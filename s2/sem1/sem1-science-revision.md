## Section A: Atoms & the Periodic Table

### Key facts

- **Inside an atom:** Protons (charge +1) and neutrons (no charge) sit in the nucleus. Electrons (charge −1) move in shells around it.
- **Two numbers:** atomic number = protons · In a neutral atom, electrons = protons. · mass number = protons + neutrons
- **Filling shells:** For the first 20 elements the shells hold 2, 8, 8, 2. Example: nitrogen (7) is 2,5.
- **Reading the table:** Group number = how many electrons are in the outer shell. Period = how many shells are in use. Group 0, the noble gases, have a full outer shell.
- **Trends:** Group 1 metals react by *losing* their single outer electron and get more reactive further down. Group 7 non-metals react by *gaining* one electron and get less reactive further down.

## Section B: Bonding & Ions

### Key facts

- **Ionic bonding:** A metal reacts with a non-metal. Electrons move from the metal atom to the non-metal atom, and the oppositely charged ions attract.
- **Ion charges:** Groups 1, 2, 3 lose electrons: 1+, 2+, 3+. Groups 5, 6, 7 gain electrons: 3−, 2−, 1−. Either way the ion ends up with a full outer shell.
- **Writing formulae:** Total + charge = total − charge. Ca²⁺ with Cl⁻ gives CaCl₂ (one 2+ balances two 1−).
- **Covalent bonding:** Between non-metal atoms, which share pairs of electrons. A single bond is one shared pair and a double bond is two. Examples: H₂O, CO₂, CH₄.
- **Metallic bonding:** Positive metal ions sit in a “sea” of delocalised electrons. That is why metals conduct and can be bent into shape.
- **Ionic compounds:** A giant lattice of ions with high melting points. They conduct electricity only when melted or dissolved in water.

## Section C: Plant Biology

### Key facts

- **Photosynthesis:** carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen · Light energy, absorbed by chlorophyll, is written above the arrow.
- **Where it happens:** In the chloroplasts of leaf cells. Carbon dioxide comes in from the air, and water comes up from the roots.
- **Using the glucose:** Glucose is used for respiration and is turned into starch, cellulose, proteins, and fats and oils.
- **Minerals:** Magnesium is part of the chlorophyll molecule. Nitrate is needed to make proteins. Fertilisers add both to the soil to raise the yield.
- **Investigation 1: light:** Pondweed in water with a lamp at different distances. Count the oxygen bubbles per minute. Keep the pondweed, the water temperature and the timing the same.
- **Investigation 2: starch test:** Boil the leaf in water, warm it in ethanol, rinse it, then add iodine solution.

## Section D: Density

### Key facts

- **The formula:** ρ = m ÷ V · Rearranged: m = ρ × V, V = m ÷ ρ. Units: g/cm³.
- **Finding volume:** Regular block: length × width × height. Irregular object: lower it into a measuring cylinder; the rise in level is its volume (1 mL = 1 cm³).
- **Units:** 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³. Water is 1 g/cm³.
- **Float or sink:** Less dense than the liquid: floats. Denser: sinks. Liquids that do not mix stack in layers by density.
- **Heating and cooling:** Heated particles move faster and spread out. The mass stays the same while the volume grows, so the density falls. Gases are far less dense than solids and liquids.
- **Water is odd:** Ice (0.92 g/cm³) is less dense than liquid water, because hydrogen bonds hold the molecules in an open pattern. Lakes freeze from the top down.

## Section E: Waves & Sound

### Key facts

- **Two kinds of wave:** **Transverse**: vibrations at right angles to the direction of travel (crests and troughs), e.g. light. **Longitudinal**: vibrations along the direction of travel (compressions and rarefactions), e.g. sound.
- **Measuring a wave:** Amplitude: middle line to a crest. Wavelength: one crest to the next. Frequency: waves per second, in hertz (Hz).
- **Period:** T = 1 ÷ f and f = 1 ÷ T, with T in seconds.
- **What you hear:** Bigger amplitude: louder. Higher frequency: higher pitch.
- **Superposition:** Crest meets crest: the amplitudes add. Crest meets trough: they cancel.
- **Sound needs a medium:** Sound is passed along by vibrating particles, so it travels through solids, liquids and gases but not through a vacuum.

## Section F: Thermal Energy

### Key facts

- **Heat vs temperature:** Temperature (°C) says how hot something is: the *average* kinetic energy of its particles. Heat, or thermal energy (J), is the *total*, so it also depends on the mass.
- **Which way?:** Heat always flows from the hotter object to the colder one, until both reach the same temperature.
- **Conduction:** Vibrating particles pass energy to their neighbours. Best in solids, especially metals.
- **Convection:** In liquids and gases: warm fluid expands, becomes less dense and rises; cooler fluid sinks to take its place.
- **Radiation:** Infrared waves. It needs no particles, so it crosses empty space.
- **Insulation & evaporation:** Insulators trap still air (wool, foam, double glazing). Evaporation happens at the surface at any temperature and cools the liquid left behind. Boiling happens all through a liquid at its boiling point.

## Section G: Electricity

### Key facts

- **Three quantities:** Voltage V, in volts (V): the push. Current I, in amps (A): the flow of charge. Resistance R, in ohms (Ω): how hard it is for current to flow.
- **Ohm’s law:** V = I × R · so I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I.
- **Series:** One loop. The current is the same everywhere. The cell’s voltage is shared between the components. R = R₁ + R₂ + …
- **Parallel:** Several branches. Each branch gets the full voltage. The branch currents add up to the current from the cell.
- **Variable resistor:** Turning its resistance up lets less current flow, so a lamp in series with it gets dimmer.
- **Meters:** An ammeter measures current; a voltmeter measures voltage.
