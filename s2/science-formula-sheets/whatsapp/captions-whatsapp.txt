SHEET 1 – Atomic Structure & Periodic Table
Atomic number = protons = electrons (in a neutral atom). Mass number = protons + neutrons, so neutrons = mass number − atomic number. Electrons fill shells 2, 8, 8 (sodium = 2,8,1; chlorine = 2,8,7). Groups are columns (outer electrons), periods are rows (number of shells).

SHEET 2 – Ions & Ionic Formulae
Groups 1–3 lose electrons and become positive ions; groups 6–7 gain electrons (8 minus the group number) and become negative ions. To write a formula, balance the charges so total positive = total negative: Mg²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ gives MgCl₂, and 2Al³⁺ + 3O²⁻ gives Al₂O₃. Ionic = transfer, covalent = sharing, metallic = sea of electrons.

SHEET 3 – Photosynthesis
Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, with light energy above the arrow (chlorophyll in the chloroplasts absorbs it). CO₂ comes from the air, water from the roots. The plant uses glucose for respiration, cellulose, starch, proteins and fats. Test: iodine turns blue-black when starch is present.

SHEET 4 – Density
ρ = m ÷ V. Rearranged: m = ρ × V and V = m ÷ ρ (g and cm³ give g/cm³). Irregular solid: volume = water level rise. 1 mL = 1 cm³, 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³, 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³. Less dense than the liquid floats, denser sinks. Heating lowers density, cooling raises it. Ice floats because it is less dense than liquid water.

SHEET 5 – Waves, Sound & Thermal Energy
T = 1 ÷ f (period in seconds, frequency in hertz). Bigger amplitude = louder; higher frequency = higher pitch. Constructive interference adds amplitudes, destructive cancels them. Heat (joules) flows from hot to cold by conduction (contact), convection (moving fluids) or radiation (infrared, works in a vacuum).

SHEET 6 – Electricity
Voltage is the push the cell gives the charge (volts, voltmeter in parallel); current is the flow of charge (amperes, ammeter in series). V = I × R, so I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I (volts, amperes, ohms). Series: same current, voltage divides, resistances add up. Parallel: same voltage on every branch, current divides. Check: 12 V and 3 A gives 4 Ω.

DIAGRAM – Anatomy of a Wave
Anatomy of a wave. The crest is the highest point and the trough the lowest. Amplitude is the height from the rest line to a crest (bigger = louder). Wavelength is the distance from crest to crest. Period T is the time for one whole wave to pass, frequency f is how many pass each second (higher = higher pitch), and T = 1 ÷ f, f = 1 ÷ T. Example: T = 0.25 s gives f = 4 Hz. Sound is longitudinal: particles bunch into compressions and spread into rarefactions.

DIAGRAM – Formula Triangles
Formula triangles. Cover the quantity you want and read off what is left. Density: m on top, ρ and V below, so ρ = m ÷ V, m = ρ × V, V = m ÷ ρ. Ohm's law: V on top, I and R below, so V = I × R, I = V ÷ R, R = V ÷ I. For waves, period and frequency are inverses: T = 1 ÷ f and f = 1 ÷ T. Worked examples: m = 120 g and V = 40 cm³ give ρ = 3 g/cm³; T = 0.25 s gives f = 4 Hz; V = 12 V and I = 3 A give R = 4 Ω.

DIAGRAM – How Heat Travels
How heat travels. Conduction: particles vibrate and pass energy on by direct contact, mainly in solids (metals best), so a spoon warms in hot soup. Convection: warm fluid is less dense and rises while cool, denser fluid sinks, making a loop in liquids and gases. Radiation: infrared waves carry heat and need no particles, so it works through a vacuum (the Sun, a campfire). Insulation and double glazing trap air, a poor conductor, so less heat escapes. Heat (joules) flows from hot to cold; temperature (°C) is the average particle energy.

DIAGRAM – Phone Charger: The Five Stages
Inside a phone charger, step 1: the five stages. The wall gives 220 V AC that swings + and − 50 times a second. A rectifier (diodes) flips the − half up and a capacitor smooths it to about 310 V DC. A transistor switches that on and off about 100,000 times a second, making 310 V pulses. A transformer steps them down to about 5 V pulses, then a diode and capacitor smooth them into steady 5 V DC for the phone.

DIAGRAM – Phone Charger: The Transformer
Inside a phone charger, step 2: the transformer. A changing magnetic field flows round the core and drives current in coil 2. Coil 1 (primary) has many turns of thin wire (e.g. 44) and takes in about 310 V pulses; coil 2 (secondary) has few turns of thicker wire (e.g. 1) and gives out about 5 V. Vp ÷ Vs = Np ÷ Ns: fewer turns on coil 2 means lower voltage (step-down). Old chargers used a big, heavy 50 Hz transformer; modern ones switch very fast so a tiny core does the same job.
