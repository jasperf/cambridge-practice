SHEET 1 – Atomic Structure & Periodic Table
Atomic number = protons = electrons (in a neutral atom). Mass number = protons + neutrons, so neutrons = mass number − atomic number. Electrons fill shells 2, 8, 8 (sodium = 2,8,1; chlorine = 2,8,7). Groups are columns (outer electrons), periods are rows (number of shells).

SHEET 2 – Ions & Ionic Formulae
Groups 1–3 lose electrons and become positive ions; groups 6–7 gain electrons (8 minus the group number) and become negative ions. To write a formula, balance the charges so total positive = total negative: Mg²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ gives MgCl₂, and 2Al³⁺ + 3O²⁻ gives Al₂O₃. Ionic = transfer, covalent = sharing, metallic = sea of electrons.

SHEET 3 – Photosynthesis
Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, with light energy above the arrow (chlorophyll in the chloroplasts absorbs it). CO₂ comes from the air, water from the roots. The plant uses glucose for respiration, cellulose, starch, proteins and fats. Test: iodine turns blue-black when starch is present.

SHEET 4 – Density
ρ = m ÷ V. Rearranged: m = ρ × V and V = m ÷ ρ (g and cm³ give g/cm³). Irregular solid: volume = water level rise. 1 mL = 1 cm³, 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³, 1 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³. Less dense than the liquid floats, denser sinks. Heating lowers density, cooling raises it. Ice floats because it is less dense than liquid water.

SHEET 5 – Waves, Sound & Thermal Energy
T = 1 ÷ f (period in seconds, frequency in hertz). Bigger amplitude = louder; higher frequency = higher pitch. Constructive interference adds amplitudes, destructive cancels them. Heat (joules) flows from hot to cold by conduction (contact), convection (moving fluids) or radiation (infrared, works in a vacuum).

SHEET 6 – Electricity
Voltage is the push the cell gives the charge (volts, voltmeter in parallel); current is the flow of charge (amperes, ammeter in series). V = I × R, so I = V ÷ R and R = V ÷ I (volts, amperes, ohms). Series: same current, voltage divides, resistances add up. Parallel: same voltage on every branch, current divides. Check: 12 V and 3 A gives 4 Ω.

DIAGRAM – Anatomy of a Wave
Anatomy of a wave. The crest is the highest point and the trough the lowest. Amplitude is the height from the rest line to a crest (bigger = louder). Wavelength is the distance from crest to crest. Period T is the time for one whole wave to pass, frequency f is how many pass each second (higher = higher pitch), and T = 1 ÷ f, f = 1 ÷ T. Example: T = 0.25 s gives f = 4 Hz. Sound is longitudinal: particles bunch into compressions and spread into rarefactions.

DIAGRAM – Formula Triangles
Formula triangles. Cover the quantity you want and read off what is left. Density: m on top, ρ and V below, so ρ = m ÷ V, m = ρ × V, V = m ÷ ρ. Ohm's law: V on top, I and R below, so V = I × R, I = V ÷ R, R = V ÷ I. For waves, period and frequency are inverses: T = 1 ÷ f and f = 1 ÷ T. Worked examples: m = 120 g and V = 40 cm³ give ρ = 3 g/cm³; T = 0.25 s gives f = 4 Hz; V = 12 V and I = 3 A give R = 4 Ω.

DIAGRAM – How Heat Travels
How heat travels. Conduction: particles vibrate and pass energy on by direct contact, mainly in solids (metals best), so a spoon warms in hot soup. Convection: warm fluid is less dense and rises while cool, denser fluid sinks, making a loop in liquids and gases. Radiation: infrared waves carry heat and need no particles, so it works through a vacuum (the Sun, a campfire). Insulation and double glazing trap air, a poor conductor, so less heat escapes. Heat (joules) flows from hot to cold; temperature (°C) is the average particle energy.

DIAGRAM – Phone Charger: The Five Stages
Inside a phone charger, step 1: the five stages. The wall gives 220 V AC that swings + and − 50 times a second. A rectifier (diodes) flips the − half up and a capacitor smooths it to about 310 V DC. A transistor switches that on and off about 100,000 times a second, making 310 V pulses. A transformer steps them down to about 5 V pulses, then a diode and capacitor smooth them into steady 5 V DC for the phone.

DIAGRAM – Phone Charger: The Transformer
Inside a phone charger, step 2: the transformer. A changing magnetic field flows round the core and drives current in coil 2. Coil 1 (primary) has many turns of thin wire (e.g. 44) and takes in about 310 V pulses; coil 2 (secondary) has few turns of thicker wire (e.g. 1) and gives out about 5 V. Vp ÷ Vs = Np ÷ Ns: fewer turns on coil 2 means lower voltage (step-down). Old chargers used a big, heavy 50 Hz transformer; modern ones switch very fast so a tiny core does the same job.

DIAGRAM – Phone Charger: Ohm's Law at the Phone
Inside a phone charger, step 3: Ohm's law at the phone. The charger from step 1 gives out a steady 5 V and the phone draws 2 A. We want R, so cover R in the triangle: V is over I, so R = V ÷ I = 5 ÷ 2 = 2.5 Ω. Check: I × R = 2 A × 2.5 Ω = 5 V, which matches the charger. Simplified: a phone isn't a fixed resistor, because the current it draws changes as the battery fills, so 2.5 Ω is its effective resistance at that moment.

DIAGRAM – The Periodic Table (Elements 1–20)
The periodic table for elements 1–20. Each box shows the atomic number, symbol, name and electronic structure. Groups are columns: the group number is the number of outer electrons, which is the last number in the structure. Periods are rows: the period number is the number of shells, which is how many numbers there are. Example: 2,8,3 has three numbers, so Period 3, and ends in 3, so Group 3: aluminium. Metals (orange) sit left of the staircase line and lose electrons to form positive ions; non-metals (blue) gain or share electrons. Group 8 (also called Group 0 or 18) holds the noble gases, with a full outer shell.

DIAGRAM – P = I × V: Examples & Practice
Power in watts. P = I × V: power (watts) = current (amps) × voltage (volts), and 1 W is 1 joule of energy every second. Formula triangle with P on top and I and V below: P = I × V, I = P ÷ V, V = P ÷ I. Change kW to W first (1 kW = 1000 W). Three worked examples with circuit diagrams: a phone on 5 V drawing 2 A uses 10 W; a 2300 W kettle on 230 V draws 10 A; a 48 W headlamp drawing 4 A runs on 12 V. DIAGRAM – Volts, Amps, Ohms and Watts in a Wire
Volts, amps, ohms and watts in a wire, side by side with water in a pipe. The battery is like a pump: voltage V (volts) is the push that gives each electron energy. Current I (amps) is how many electrons pass a point each second, like litres of water per second. Resistance R (ohms) is how hard it is to get through: atoms in the resistor get in the way, like a narrow pipe, and the wire heats up. Power P (watts) = I × V is the energy handed over each second. Electrons move from − to +, while the current arrow points + to −. Electrons are not used up: in one loop the current is the same everywhere; what they lose is energy. V = I × R and P = I × V.

DIAGRAM – Electrons Are Delivery Vans
Electrons are delivery vans on a loop road. The battery is the depot: it loads every van (electron) with a parcel of energy. The vans carry the energy round the circuit and drop it off at the bulb, where it comes out as light and heat. The same empty vans drive back to the battery to pick up more. Vans are never used up, only the parcels, so the current is the same everywhere in one loop. Voltage V is the energy loaded on each van (1 V = 1 joule per coulomb), current I is how many vans pass a point each second, and power P = I × V is the energy dropped off each second. Example: 2 A × 6 V = 12 W.

DIAGRAM – From Cell to Gene
Zoom in from a cell to a gene. The nucleus is the control centre of the cell and holds 46 chromosomes. Each chromosome is one long, coiled molecule of DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid). DNA is a double helix: two strands twisted like a spiral ladder, a shape worked out by Watson and Crick in 1953. A gene is a short section of DNA that codes for one characteristic, such as eye colour. Every body cell has 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs: one of each pair comes from Mum and the other from Dad, so you look like both parents but are not identical to either.

DIAGRAM – Mitosis and Meiosis
Two ways a cell divides, shown with a model cell of 4 chromosomes (a human body cell has 46). Mitosis: 1 division gives 2 cells, identical to the parent, each with the full number (diploid, 2n, 46 in humans). It is used for growth and repair. Meiosis: 2 divisions give 4 cells, all different, each with half the number (haploid, n, 23 in humans). It makes the gametes: sperm in the testes and eggs in the ovaries. At fertilisation a sperm (23) and an egg (23) join to make a zygote (46). Without meiosis the number would double every generation (46 + 46 = 92). Other animals: cat 38 → 19, horse 64 → 32, chimpanzee 48 → 24.

DIAGRAM – XX and XY: Boy or Girl?
Boy or girl? Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes: 22 pairs of autosomes and 1 pair of sex chromosomes. Females are XX and males are XY; the Y is much smaller than the X. Every egg carries an X. Half of the sperm carry an X and half carry a Y, so the father's sperm decides. X sperm + X egg gives XX, a girl; Y sperm + X egg gives XY, a boy. The 2 × 2 grid gives 2 girls and 2 boys, a 50% chance of each. Every fertilisation is a new 50 : 50 chance, whatever earlier children were.

DIAGRAM – Mother and Fetus
The fetus grows in the uterus (womb) for about 9 months, inside the amniotic fluid. The umbilical cord joins it to the placenta, where substances pass between Mum's blood and the baby's blood without the two mixing. Oxygen and food go to the fetus; carbon dioxide and waste go back to Mum. Harmful things get through too: nicotine, carbon monoxide, alcohol, drugs and listeria bacteria. Diet: eat a balanced diet; a poor diet means the fetus grows slowly and the baby is born small and less able to fight disease; avoid soft cheese (listeria) and cut down on caffeine. Smoking: carbon monoxide means less oxygen, the fetus's heart beats faster, and the baby may be premature, have a low birth weight or be stillborn. Alcohol and drugs: heavy drinking can cause fetal alcohol syndrome (small head, smooth upper lip, small eye openings); a baby can be born addicted to drugs.
