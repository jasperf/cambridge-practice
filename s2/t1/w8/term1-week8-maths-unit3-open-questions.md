# Secondary 2 · Term 1 · Mathematics — Unit 3 Practice Test: 20 Open Questions

**Stage 9 · Unit 3 (3.1–3.4)**
**20 questions · 25 marks**

One more practice test on the whole of Unit 3: powers of 10, multiplying and dividing decimals, compound percentages, and bounds (with significant figures). Every question is open, with no multiple-choice options to guess from. Part A is ten quick one-line questions, one skill each. Part B is five short stories, where you have to decide which skill a question needs before you use it. This document contains all questions from the interactive sheet in a printable format. Students write their answers directly in their notebooks.

---

## Part A: Quick Lines

**Toolkit:**

- **Powers of 10** — a negative power makes a multiplication smaller and a division bigger.
- **Decimals** — change the calculation into an equivalent one with whole numbers.
- **Percentages** — turn each change into a multiplier.
- **Bounds** — find the unit of rounding, halve it, and ask what kind of number was rounded.

### Questions

**Q1** (1 mark)
Work out 0.072 × 10⁴

**Q2** (1 mark)
Work out 5.6 ÷ 10⁻²

**Q3** (1 mark)
Find the missing power: 830 × 10^□ = 0.83

**Q4** (1 mark)
Work out 0.6 × 0.07

**Q5** (1 mark)
Work out 2.4 ÷ 0.08

**Q6** (1 mark)
Write the multiplier for a **6% decrease**.

**Q7** (1 mark)
Round 0.004 739 to 2 significant figures.

**Q8** (1 mark)
A pencil is 7.6 cm long, correct to 1 decimal place. What is the **lower** bound of its length?

**Q9** (1 mark)
A **whole number** is rounded to the nearest 100. The answer is 900. What is the upper bound?

**Q10** (1 mark)
A tower is 340 m tall, correct to 2 significant figures. What is the **upper** bound of its height?

## Part B: Story Questions

**Before you calculate:** read the whole story, pick out the numbers you need, and decide which Unit 3 skill the question is really asking for. Some stories hide a trap for anyone who uses the numbers straight from the text.

### Story 1 — Under the Microscope

In the science lab, Leo's microscope makes everything look **10³ times** wider than it really is.

**Q11** (1 mark)
A plant cell is really 0.025 mm wide. How wide does it look under Leo's microscope?

**Q12** (1 mark)
Through the same microscope, a bacterium *looks* 2 mm wide. How wide is it **really**?

### Story 2 — The Sourdough Bakery

Amira runs a small bakery. Each sourdough loaf uses **0.35 kg** of flour, and her flour costs **$1.20 per kg**.

**Q13** (2 marks)
Amira opens an 8.4 kg sack of flour. How many loaves can she make from it?

**Q14** (1 mark)
How much does the flour in **one** loaf cost, in dollars?

### Story 3 — The Double Discount

A phone costs **$480**. On Friday the shop takes **25% off**. On Monday it takes a **further 10% off** the Friday price.

**Q15** (2 marks)
What is the price of the phone on Monday?

**Q16** (2 marks)
Jake says: *"25% off and then 10% off is 35% off altogether."* What percentage of the original $480 has **really** been taken off?

### Story 4 — Going Viral

Nina posts a video of her cat walking across a piano. It has **2000 views**. Her friend predicts that the number of views will go up by **40% every day**.

**Q17** (1 mark)
Using the prediction, how many views will the video have after **3 days**?

**Q18** (2 marks)
After how many days does the prediction first go *above* 10 000 views? You can use a calculator.

*On the interactive sheet, once Q17 and Q18 are answered, an animated bar chart grows the views one day at a time against a 10 000 target line.*

### Story 5 — The Goods Lift

A warehouse lift has a sign: **Maximum load 450 kg**, correct to the nearest 10 kg. Sam has to move crates of bottles. Each crate weighs **30 kg**, correct to the nearest kg.

Sam must be *certain* the lift is never overloaded.

**Q19** (1 mark)
What is the **lowest** the real maximum load of the lift could be?

**Q20** (2 marks)
Sam works out 450 ÷ 30 = 15 and plans to load 15 crates. What is the **largest** number of crates Sam can load and still be *certain* the lift is safe?

*On the interactive sheet, once Q19 and Q20 are answered, an animation loads the crates one at a time at their heaviest possible mass, next to a meter showing the lowest possible limit.*
