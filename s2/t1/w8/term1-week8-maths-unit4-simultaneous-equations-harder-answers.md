**Q30:** a) ×2 and ×3: 4x + 6y = 26 and 15x − 6y = 12. Different signs, so add: 19x = 38, x = 2. Then 4 + 3y = 13, y = 3.
b) ×5 and ×2: 10x + 15y = 65 and 10x − 4y = 8. Same signs, so subtract: 15y − (−4y) = 19y and 65 − 8 = 57, so y = 3. Then 2x + 9 = 13, x = 2.
c) Getting rid of *y* first: the multipliers (2 and 3) and the numbers (26, 12) are smaller, and adding is easier than subtracting a negative. Any sensible reason is fine.

**Q31:** (1) Line up. (2) Remove *x*: ×3 and ×2 give 12x − 9y = 3 and 12x + 10y = 98. (3) Same signs, so subtract: −9y − 10y = −19y and 3 − 98 = −95. (4) −19y = −95, so y = 5. (5) 4x − 15 = 1, so x = 4. Check: 6 × 4 + 5 × 5 = 24 + 25 = 49 ✓. (Removing *y* with ×5 and ×3, then adding, gives 38x = 152 and the same answer.)

**Q32:** a) Multiplying keeps the equation balanced only if *both sides* are multiplied; if the number after = is left alone, the new equation is false and the answer comes out wrong. b) Look at the signs of the two matching terms: same signs subtract, different signs add, because that is what makes them cancel to zero.

**Q33:** a) Multiply the second by 2: 4I₁ − 10I₂ = −8. Subtract from 4I₁ + 3I₂ = 18: 13I₂ = 26, so I₂ = 2 A. Then 4I₁ + 6 = 18, I₁ = 3 A.
b) 4 × 3 + 3 × 2 = 18 ✓ and 2 × 3 − 5 × 2 = −4 ✓.
c) 3 + 2 = 5 A.

**Q34:** a) Adding: 2R_A = 960, so R_A = 480 N and R_B = 240 N.
b) Trestle A pushes harder, because the painter stands nearer to A (2 m from A, 4 m from B), so A carries more of the weight.
c) Trestle B, because the painter is now nearer to B.

**Q35:** a) Multiply the first by 2: 2x + 4y = 22. Add 3x − 4y = 3: 5x = 25, x = 5. Then 5 + 2y = 11, y = 3. The paths cross at (5, 3). Check: 15 − 12 = 3 ✓.
b) Lines through (1, 5) and (5, 3) for P, and (1, 0) and (5, 3) for Q, crossing at (5, 3).
c) So it can stop one robot or change their timing, and the robots do not collide at the crossing point.

**Q36:** Many answers. Example: 5x + 3y = 9 and 2x − 7y = 20 (5 × 3 + 3 × (−2) = 9 and 2 × 3 − 7 × (−2) = 20). Removing *y* needs ×7 and ×3: 35x + 21y = 63 and 6x − 21y = 60; adding gives 41x = 123, x = 3, then y = −2. Check the student's own pair by putting x = 3, y = −2 into both equations.
